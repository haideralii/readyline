# Verification or certification: which NDIS audit do you need?

**The short answer: your registration groups decide.** Every support type you register for (a "registration group" or "class", with a four-digit code) is listed by the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission as either lower-risk or higher-risk. Lower-risk groups get a verification audit. If any one of your groups is higher-risk, your whole registration gets a certification audit.

You do not choose. An auditor does not choose either. The list below is the Commission's own, and the free questionnaire works it out for your exact mix of groups, with the source shown.

## The two audits in plain words

**Verification** is a desk audit. An approved auditor reads your documents against the verification module of the Practice Standards: human resource management, incident management, complaints management and risk management. Nobody visits you, and nobody speaks to the people you support. It is the quicker and usually the cheaper audit.

**Certification** is a full audit. The auditor reads your documents against the core module (and any supplementary modules your groups add), visits your business, and talks to your workers and to some participants. Certified providers also face a mid-term audit about 18 months after registration.

## Four cases the list alone does not settle

- **Restrictive practices.** If you use, or plan to use, any regulated restrictive practice, the implementing behaviour support plans module applies to you whatever your groups.
- **Supported independent living (class 0138).** The Commission has two questions that decide whether a service is SIL at all. If your service is a few hours a week, or the participant manages their own workers, it may not be SIL.
- **Early childhood, as an individual or partnership.** A provider who only delivers early childhood supports as a sole trader or partnership gets a modified certification.
- **Specialist disability accommodation only.** An SDA-only provider is assessed against the SDA module without the core module.

The questionnaire asks about each of these, so the result covers them.

## The mid-term audit

If your audit is certification, the Commission requires a mid-term audit about 18 months after registration. It covers the governance and operational management part of the core module, plus any standard that had a corrective action at your first audit. Start preparing from month 12. Verification registrations have no mid-term audit.

## When registration becomes compulsory

Registration is becoming compulsory in waves. Supported independent living and digital platform providers were first, from 1 July 2026. Providers of higher-risk activities such as personal care are next, from 1 July 2027, with every in-scope provider registered by December 2030.

## FAQ

### Can I pick verification to save money?

No. The audit type follows your registration groups. If you only deliver lower-risk supports, you get verification. Add one higher-risk group, such as personal care, and the whole registration moves to certification.

### Does a sole trader need a certification audit?

Only if a group you register for is higher-risk. A sole trader doing household tasks (0120) alone gets verification. A sole trader doing personal care (0107) gets certification, with a site visit and interviews.

### What does the auditor read in a verification audit?

Your documents for the four verification-module standards: human resource management (worker screening and training), incident management, complaints management and risk management. The self-assessment and the evidence you attach to it are the core of it.

### How long does registration last?

Three years. A renewal audit happens before it expires. Certified providers also have the mid-term audit at about 18 months.

### What happens if I add a service later?

Tell the Commission through a change to your registration. If the new group is higher-risk and your registration was verification, expect a certification audit. UNVERIFIED: the exact change process; check the Commission's "change your registration" guidance.

### Who does the audit?

An approved quality auditor, chosen and paid by you, from the Commission's list. We are not an auditor and take no fee from any auditor.
