# Study: Australian Consumer Law (Competition and Consumer Act 2010 (Cth), Schedule 2)

Compilation No. 167, C2026C00405, 16 September 2026. Volume 4 of the compilation, https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00109/2026-09-16/2026-09-16/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_4/document_4.html. Read 16 Oct 2026. All sections below VERIFIED unless marked.

## How the ACL reaches a sole trader

Schedule 2 applies "as a law of the Commonwealth to the conduct of corporations" (CCA s 131(1), volume 3). Readyline trades as an individual, so two other routes matter. CCA s 6(3) gives Parts 2-1, 3-1, 4-1 and others of the ACL the effect they would have if confined to conduct that "involves the use of postal, telegraphic or telephonic services" and if a reference to a corporation "included a reference to a person not being a corporation"; s 6(3A) does the same for Part 2-3 (unfair terms) for "contracts for or relating to: (i) the use of postal, telegraphic or telephonic services". A website and an email are such services, so ss 18, 23 and 29 reach Readyline's pages as Commonwealth law. The state application law (CCA Part XIAA s 140 "application law"; for NSW the Fair Trading Act 1987 (NSW), UNVERIFIED, not reached) applies the ACL to everyone else. The consumer guarantees in Part 3-2 reach a sole trader through the state application law; gist: the guarantees apply either way.

## s 2, goods

"goods includes ... (e) computer software". A generated `.docx` policy is a product of a service rather than software; the guarantee that fits is s 61 ("any product resulting from the services").

## s 3, meaning of consumer

s 3(3): a person acquires services as a consumer "if, and only if: (a) the amount paid or payable for the services ... did not exceed: (i) $40,000; or (ii) if a greater amount is prescribed ... that greater amount; or (b) the services were of a kind ordinarily acquired for personal, domestic or household use". Reg 77A of the Competition and Consumer Regulations 2010 (compilation No. 100, F2026C00856): "the amount of $100,000 is prescribed". Gist: a provider buying a $349 to $499 pack or a $29 to $49 subscription for its business is a consumer; the guarantees apply though the buyer is a business.

## s 4, future matters

"If: (a) a person makes a representation with respect to any future matter ...; and (b) the person does not have reasonable grounds for making the representation; the representation is taken ... to be misleading", and under s 4(2) the maker "is taken not to have had reasonable grounds ... unless evidence is adduced to the contrary". Gist: "keeps you audit-ready", "documents stay available for 12 months", "we reply within two business days" are future-matter representations that need grounds Readyline can show.

## s 18, misleading or deceptive conduct

"A person must not, in trade or commerce, engage in conduct that is misleading or deceptive or is likely to mislead or deceive." No exclusion by contract; a disclaimer is part of the conduct, not a cure.

## ss 23 to 28, unfair contract terms

s 23(1): a term of "a consumer contract or small business contract is void if: (a) the term is unfair; and (b) the contract is a standard form contract". s 23(2A): a person "contravenes this subsection if ... the person proposed the unfair term", with a pecuniary penalty (note to s 23(2A), s 224). s 23(3): a consumer contract is a supply "to an individual whose acquisition ... is wholly or predominantly for personal, domestic or household use". s 23(4): a small business contract is one where "at least one party ... (i) ... employs fewer than 100 persons; (ii) [or has] turnover ... less than $10,000,000". s 24(1): unfair means "(a) it would cause a significant imbalance ...; and (b) it is not reasonably necessary in order to protect the legitimate interests of the party who would be advantaged ...; and (c) it would cause detriment"; s 24(2) the court "must take into account ... (a) the extent to which the term is transparent; (b) the contract as a whole"; s 24(4) presumes a term "not to be reasonably necessary ... unless that party proves otherwise". s 25 examples include "(a) ... permitting ... one party (but not another party) to avoid or limit performance", "(b) ... to terminate the contract", "(d) ... to vary the terms of the contract", "(k) a term that limits ... one party's right to sue another party". s 26(1): s 23 does not apply to a term that "(a) ... defines the main subject matter" or "(b) ... sets the upfront price". s 27(1): a contract alleged to be standard form "is presumed to be a standard form contract unless another party ... proves otherwise". Gist: Readyline's terms are a standard form small business contract with every customer; the one-sided variation, suspension and liability-cap terms are the ones to test.

## s 29, false or misleading representations

s 29(1): a person must not "in connection with the supply or possible supply of goods or services or in connection with the promotion ... (b) make a false or misleading representation that services are of a particular standard, quality, value or grade; ... (g) ... that goods or services have sponsorship, approval, performance characteristics, accessories, uses or benefits; or (h) ... that the person making the representation has a sponsorship, approval or affiliation; ... (m) ... concerning the existence, exclusion or effect of any condition, warranty, guarantee, right or remedy". Gist: "audit-ready", "aligned to the Practice Standards", a refund page that reads as the whole of the buyer's rights, and any hint of Commission approval are each a s 29 question; pecuniary penalty (note 1).

## ss 54, 60 to 63, the guarantees

s 60: "there is a guarantee that the services will be rendered with due care and skill". s 61(1): where the consumer "makes known to the supplier any particular purpose", "the services, and any product resulting from the services, will be reasonably fit for that purpose"; s 61(2): where the consumer makes known "the result that the consumer wishes the services to achieve", the services and product "might reasonably be expected to achieve that result"; s 61(3): not where "it was unreasonable for the consumer to rely on, the skill or judgment of the supplier". s 62: supply "within a reasonable time" where no time is fixed. s 54 (acceptable quality) is a goods guarantee; noted for the `.docx` files only if they are ever treated as goods. Gist: the questionnaire makes the purpose known (passing a named audit), so s 61 is live; the general-information statement goes to s 61(3) reliance but does not exclude the guarantee.

## ss 64 and 64A, exclusion and limitation

s 64(1): a term "is void to the extent that the term purports to exclude, restrict or modify ... (c) any liability of a person for a failure to comply with a guarantee". s 64A(2): for services "other than services of a kind ordinarily acquired for personal, domestic or household use", a term "is not void under section 64 merely because the term limits the person's liability for failure to comply with a guarantee to: (a) the supplying of the services again; or (b) the payment of the cost of having the services supplied again"; s 64A(3): not where the buyer "establishes that it is not fair or reasonable" to rely on it, with s 64A(4) factors (bargaining strength, inducement, knowledge of the term). Gist: a cap at "the amount you paid in the 12 months" is not one of the two s 64A(2) forms; it survives only to the extent it limits liability outside the guarantees.

## ss 259 to 260, 267 to 269, remedies

s 267(2): a failure that "can be remedied and is not a major failure" lets the consumer "require the supplier to remedy the failure within a reasonable time"; s 267(3): a failure that "cannot be remedied or is a major failure" lets the consumer "(a) terminate the contract ...; or (b) ... recover compensation for any reduction in the value"; s 267(4): damages for loss "if it was reasonably foreseeable". s 268(1)(a): a major failure where "the services would not have been acquired by a reasonable consumer fully acquainted with the nature and extent of the failure"; (c) and (d) where they are unfit for the purpose or result made known and "cannot, easily and within a reasonable time, be remedied". s 269(3): on termination the consumer recovers "a refund of: (a) any money paid ... to the extent that the consumer has not already consumed the services". ss 259 and 260 are the parallel for goods. Gist: the refund page's "we decide in your favour" sits above these; the page must not read as the ceiling.

## Not relied on

ss 134 (information standards), 139 (manufacturer liability to non-injured persons) and 142 (defences to defective goods actions) were located in the text and do not bear on a services business; recorded so the next reader does not search again.

# Study: Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) and the Australian Privacy Principles

Compilation No. 104, C2026C00227, 4 June 2026, https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A03712/2026-06-04/2026-06-04/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html. OAIC APP Guidelines chapters B (v1.4, 21 Dec 2022), 5 (v1.2, 22 Jul 2019), 12 (v1.1, 22 Jul 2019). Read 16 Oct 2026. VERIFIED unless marked.

## s 6, definitions

"APP entity means an agency or organisation." "personal information means information or an opinion about an identified individual, or an individual who is reasonably identifiable". "sensitive information means: (a) information or an opinion about an individual's: ... (ix) criminal record; that is also personal information" (and the health, biometric and other limbs not quoted). "employee record, in relation to an employee, means a record of personal information relating to the employment of the employee", with examples including "the engagement, training, disciplining or resignation of the employee" and "the employee's performance or conduct". "holds: an entity holds personal information if the entity has possession or control of a record that contains the personal information." Gist: a worker code tied to a check number and a decision code is personal information about a reasonably identifiable individual; an exclusion or interim bar is an opinion derived from a criminal record and the sensitive-information question is open; Readyline "holds" what sits in its database, the owner's notes included.

## s 6C, organisation

An organisation "means: (a) an individual; or (b) a body corporate; ... that is not a small business operator, a registered political party, an agency, a State or Territory authority". Gist: a sole trader is an organisation unless a small business operator.

## s 6D, small business and small business operator

s 6D(1): "A business is a small business at a time ... if its annual turnover for the previous financial year is $3,000,000 or less"; s 6D(2) for a new business, "if its annual turnover for the current year is $3,000,000 or less". s 6D(3): a small business operator "carries on one or more small businesses; and (b) does not carry on a business that is not a small business". s 6D(4): not a small business operator if the entity "(b) provides a health service to another individual and holds any health information except in an employee record; or (c) discloses personal information about another individual to anyone else for a benefit, service or advantage; or (d) provides a benefit, service or advantage to collect personal information about another individual from anyone else; or (e) is a contracted service provider for a Commonwealth contract". s 6D(7): paragraph (4)(c) is not triggered where the disclosure is "(a) with the consent of the other individual; or (b) as required or authorised by or under legislation". Gist: Readyline on its stated facts is a small business operator and so not an APP entity, unless (4)(c) catches its disclosures to Anthropic, Stripe, Supabase and Vercel; whether paying a processor to handle the data is disclosing it "for a benefit, service or advantage" is a construction question the OAIC's small-business page would normally answer and it was not reached (UNVERIFIED).

## s 6DA, annual turnover

"the total of the following that is earned in the year in the course of the business: (a) the proceeds of sales of goods and/or services; ..." Gist: the $3,000,000 is gross proceeds, not profit.

## s 6E and s 6EA, treated as an organisation

s 6E(1) and (2): regulations may prescribe a small business operator, or its acts, to be treated as an organisation. s 6EA(1): "This Act applies in relation to a small business operator as if the operator were an organisation while a choice by the operator to be treated as an organisation is registered under this section"; s 6EA(2): the choice is "in writing given to the Commissioner". Gist: Readyline can opt in and make the privacy page's APP promise bind under the Act rather than only under the ACL.

## s 6FB, health service

s 6FB(1): an activity is a health service if "intended or claimed ... (a) to assess, maintain or improve the individual's health; ... (d) to treat the individual's illness, disability or injury". s 6FB(3)(b): "an activity mentioned in subsection (1) or (2) that takes place in the course of providing aged care, palliative care or care for a person with a disability is a health service". Gist: a provider delivering personal care or SIL provides a health service; with s 6D(4)(b), if it "holds any health information except in an employee record" it is an APP entity whatever its turnover. This is the provider's position, not Readyline's; it decides what the generated privacy policy must say.

## s 7B(3), employee records

"An act done, or practice engaged in, by an organisation that is or was an employer of an individual, is exempt ... if the act or practice is directly related to: (a) a current or former employment relationship between the employer and the individual; and (b) an employee record held by the organisation and relating to the individual." Gist: the exemption belongs to the employer and only for employees; a contractor or volunteer has no "employment relationship", and Readyline is nobody's employer here, so s 7B(3) never exempts Readyline and exempts the provider only for its employees.

## s 15, s 16A

s 15: "An APP entity must not do an act, or engage in a practice, that breaches an Australian Privacy Principle." s 16A: the permitted general situations table (lessening a serious threat, suspected unlawful activity, and so on; items not quoted).

## s 26WE, eligible data breach

s 26WE(1)(a): the Part applies where "an APP entity holds personal information" and is bound by APP 11.1. s 26WE(2): an eligible data breach where "(i) there is unauthorised access to, or unauthorised disclosure of, the information; (ii) a reasonable person would conclude that the access or disclosure would be likely to result in serious harm to any of the individuals". Gist: the notifiable data breaches scheme binds APP entities; a small business operator that has not opted in has no statutory notification duty, so a privacy page that says "as the law requires" says less than it seems.

## APP 1

APP 1.3: "An APP entity must have a clearly expressed and up-to-date policy ... about the management of personal information". APP 1.4: it must contain "(a) the kinds of personal information that the entity collects and holds; (b) how ...; (c) the purposes ...; (d) how an individual may access ... and seek the correction ...; (e) how an individual may complain ... and how the entity will deal with such a complaint; (f) whether the entity is likely to disclose personal information to overseas recipients; (g) ... the countries in which such recipients are likely to be located if it is practicable to specify those countries".

## APP 3

APP 3.2: an organisation "must not collect personal information (other than sensitive information) unless the information is reasonably necessary for one or more of the entity's functions or activities". APP 3.3: sensitive information only with consent and necessity, or under 3.4. APP 3.5: "only by lawful and fair means". APP 3.6: "only from the individual unless ... (b) it is unreasonable or impracticable to do so". Guidelines B.26: an entity collects "only if the entity collects the personal information for inclusion in a record or generally available publication"; B.29: no collection "where that information is acquired but not included in a record". Gist: the one-time fill writes names into a CSV (a record) before serving it, so treating it as a collection is the safe reading; it collects from the owner, not the worker, so APP 3.6(b) must be the reason.

## APP 5

APP 5.1: "At or before the time or, if that is not practicable, as soon as practicable after, an APP entity collects personal information about an individual, the entity must take such steps (if any) as are reasonable in the circumstances: (a) to notify the individual of such matters referred to in subclause 5.2 ...; or (b) to otherwise ensure that the individual is aware". APP 5.2 matters include "(a) the identity and contact details of the APP entity; (b) ... the fact that the entity so collects ... and the circumstances of that collection; ... (d) the purposes ...; (f) any other APP entity, body or person ... to which the APP entity usually discloses personal information of the kind collected; ... (i) whether the APP entity is likely to disclose the personal information to overseas recipients; (j) ... the countries". Guidelines 5.6: reasonable steps include, where "the entity collects personal information from another entity, ensuring that the other entity has notified or made the individual aware of the relevant APP 5 matters on its behalf (such as through an enforceable contractual arrangement)". Guidelines 5.10 to 5.11: the fact and circumstances of collection "applies where either the personal information has been collected from a third party or the individual may not be aware", and the individual "should be made aware of the name of the entity" it came from. Gist: the owner's tick is the notice on Readyline's behalf; the Guidelines' example of a reliable mechanism is a contract, and the terms of use are that contract if they say so.

## APP 6

APP 6.1: information "collected for a particular purpose (the primary purpose)" must not be used or disclosed "for another purpose (the secondary purpose) unless: (a) the individual has consented ...; or (b) subclause 6.2 or 6.3 applies"; 6.2(a): where "the individual would reasonably expect" it and the secondary purpose is "related to the primary purpose" (or "directly related" for sensitive information). Gist: the auditor link and the Commission's request are secondary purposes a worker would expect once the notice names them (APP 5.2(f)).

## APP 8

APP 8.1: before disclosing to "the overseas recipient ... who is not in Australia", the entity "must take such steps as are reasonable in the circumstances to ensure that the overseas recipient does not breach the Australian Privacy Principles"; APP 8.2(a): not where the entity "reasonably believes" the recipient is bound by a substantially similar law or scheme with enforcement mechanisms; 8.2(b): not where the individual, "expressly inform[ed]" that 8.1 will not apply, consents. Gist: Anthropic (United States) and Stripe ("outside Australia") are overseas recipients of whatever personal information reaches them; APP 1.4(g) wants the countries named where practicable.

## APP 11

APP 11.1: "such steps as are reasonable in the circumstances to protect the information: (a) from misuse, interference and loss; and (b) from unauthorised access, modification or disclosure". APP 11.2: where the entity "no longer needs the information for any purpose ... and ... is not required by or under an Australian law ... to retain the information; the entity must take such steps as are reasonable ... to destroy the information or to ensure that the information is de-identified".

## APP 12

APP 12.1: "If an APP entity holds personal information about an individual, the entity must, on request by the individual, give the individual access to the information." APP 12.3 exceptions for an organisation include "(b) giving access would have an unreasonable impact on the privacy of other individuals", "(d) ... existing or anticipated legal proceedings", "(j) ... evaluative information generated within the entity in connection with a commercially sensitive decision-making process". APP 12.4: respond "within a reasonable period after the request is made" and "give access to the information in the manner requested by the individual, if it is reasonable and practicable". APP 12.8: a charge "must not be excessive and must not apply to the making of the request". APP 12.9: a refusal needs "a written notice that sets out: (a) the reasons for the refusal ...; (b) the mechanisms available to complain". Guidelines 12.3: APP 12 "does not prevent an APP entity from giving access ... under an informal administrative arrangement, provided the minimum access requirements stipulated in APP 12 have been met". Gist: a worker home that shows the worker's own rows is an informal arrangement; the owner's notes about the worker are personal information Readyline holds, and a request for them is answered under 12.1 unless 12.3 applies.

## APP 13

APP 13.1: on request, or where the entity "is satisfied that ... the information is inaccurate, out-of-date, incomplete, irrelevant or misleading", take "such steps (if any) as are reasonable ... to correct that information". APP 13.2: notify another APP entity of a correction on request "unless it is impracticable or unlawful".

# Study: National Disability Insurance Scheme Act 2013 (Cth)

Compilation No. 28, C2026C00451, 1 October 2026, includes Act No. 66 of 2026. https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2013A00020/latest/text (authorised Word text, `rev2/dl/act.txt`). Read 16 Oct 2026. VERIFIED unless marked.

## s 9, definitions

"approved quality auditor means a person or body approved by the Commissioner under section 73U." "NDIS worker screening check means an assessment, under an NDIS worker screening law, of whether a person who works, or seeks to work, with people with disability poses a risk to such people." "NDIS worker screening law means a law of a State or Territory determined in an instrument under subsection 10B(1)." "registered NDIS provider means a person or entity who is registered under section 73E." "restrictive practice means any practice or intervention that has the effect of restricting the rights or freedom of movement of a person with disability." "NDIS provider has the meaning given by section 10C." There is no definition of "worker" in s 9; the word is defined in the Worker Screening Rules s 5 and the Practice Standards Rules s 4, differently. Gist: REQ-027's "the Act's 'worker'" (Domain rules, finding 164, "VERIFY the words") has no s 9 referent; the Act's nearest words are s 73T(3)(d), "workers employed or otherwise engaged by registered NDIS providers".

## s 10C, NDIS provider

s 10C(1): a person or entity is an NDIS provider if it "(a) ... receives: (i) funding under the arrangements set out in Chapter 2; or (ii) NDIS amounts (other than as a participant); or (b) ... provides supports or services to people with disability other than under the National Disability Insurance Scheme; and ... is prescribed". Gist: an unregistered provider paid from a plan is an NDIS provider and is Code-covered (Code of Conduct Rules s 5(2)(a)).

## s 11A, key personnel

s 11A(1): "(a) a member of the group of persons who is responsible for the executive decisions of the person or entity; (b) any other person who has authority or responsibility for (or significant influence over) planning, directing or controlling the activities of the person or entity." s 11A(2): includes "a director of the body corporate" or "a member of the person's or entity's governing body". Gist: a sole trader is the one person responsible for their own executive decisions.

## s 73B, requirement to be registered

s 73B(1): the rules "may require that specified classes of supports provided under participants' plans are to be provided only by persons who are registered". s 73B(3): a person contravenes if "the person holds themselves out as being able to provide a support under a participant's plan" where the rules require registration "and the person is not so registered". Offences and civil penalties in (4) to (6). Gist: a provider's own marketing while unregistered is the provider's exposure; Readyline's pages must not put words of that kind in a provider's mouth.

## s 73E, registration

s 73E(1): the Commissioner may register where "(c) the applicant has been assessed by an approved quality auditor as meeting the applicable standards ...; and (d) the Commissioner is satisfied that the applicant is suitable ...; and (e) ... the applicant's key personnel (if any) are suitable". s 73E(5): the certificate "must specify ... (b) the classes of supports or services the person is registered to provide; ... (e) the period for which the registration is in force". Gist: the period is whatever the certificate says; three years is practice, not law.

## s 73F, conditions

s 73F(2): registration is subject to "(a) a condition that the person comply with all applicable requirements imposed by a law of the Commonwealth or a law of the State or Territory in which the person or entity operates as a registered NDIS provider; (b) ... the NDIS Code of Conduct; (c) ... all applicable standards and other requirements of the NDIS Practice Standards; (d) ... record keeping ... for the purposes of section 73Q; ... (h) ... reportable incidents ... for the purposes of section 73Z". Gist: paragraph (a) makes a state screening Act's prohibition a condition of registration; a Commonwealth Rules exception cannot lift a state offence.

## s 73T, Practice Standards

s 73T(1): the rules "may make provision for or in relation to standards concerning the quality of supports or services"; s 73T(2): "Rules made for the purposes of subsection (1) are to be known as the NDIS Practice Standards"; s 73T(3)(d): they may deal with "the screening of workers employed or otherwise engaged by registered NDIS providers or of members of the key personnel". Note to s 73T(1): non-compliance "constitutes a breach of condition of registration (see paragraph 73F(2)(c) and section 73J)".

## s 73U, approved quality auditors

"The Commissioner may, in writing, approve a person or body to be an approved quality auditor."

## s 73V, Code of Conduct

s 73V(1): a code that applies to "(a) NDIS providers; (b) persons employed or otherwise engaged by NDIS providers or members of the key personnel"; s 73V(3): a person contravenes if "subject to a requirement under the NDIS Code of Conduct; and (b) fails to comply", civil penalty "250 penalty units" or "10,000 penalty units" for a serious contravention by a provider.

## ss 73W, 73X, complaints

s 73W: a registered provider "must implement and maintain a complaints management and resolution system that: (a) is appropriate for the size of the provider". s 73X: the rules may prescribe complaints arrangements.

## ss 73Y, 73Z, incidents

s 73Y: an incident management system "appropriate for the size of the provider". s 73Z(1): the rules "must prescribe arrangements relating to registered NDIS providers notifying and managing reportable incidents that occur, or are alleged to have occurred, in connection with the provision of supports or services by registered NDIS providers". s 73Z(4): "Reportable incident means: (a) the death of a person with disability; or (b) serious injury ...; or (c) abuse or neglect ...; or (d) unlawful sexual or physical contact with, or assault of, a person with disability; or (e) sexual misconduct committed against, or in the presence of, a person with disability, including grooming ...; or (f) the use of a restrictive practice in relation to a person with disability, other than where the use is in accordance with an authorisation (however described) of a State or Territory". s 73Z(5): the rules may provide "(a) that a specified act, omission or event is a reportable incident; (b) that a specified act, omission or event is not". Gist: the duty is the registered provider's; the list is the Act's as varied by the Incident Rules s 16.

## s 73ZA, protected disclosures

Noted only: workers, contractors and participants who disclose a suspected contravention to the Commissioner or key personnel are protected.

# Study: NDIS (Provider Registration and Practice Standards) Rules 2018

Compilation No. 6, F2026C00527, 1 July 2026, includes F2026L00802. https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2018L00631/latest/text (authorised Word text, `rev2/dl/rules.txt`). Read 16 Oct 2026. VERIFIED.

## s 4, definitions

"applicable standards means the NDIS Practice Standards that apply to a specified class of supports under Part 6." "verification means an assessment by an approved quality auditor ... by conducting a desk audit of the applicant or provider, including reviewing the applicant's or provider's relevant documentation". "regulated restrictive practice means a restrictive practice that is or involves any of the following: (a) seclusion ...; (b) chemical restraint ...; (c) mechanical restraint ...; (d) physical restraint ...; (e) environmental restraints". "worker means any of the following: (a) a person employed or otherwise engaged by a registered NDIS provider; (b) a partner ... whose role is of the kind mentioned in paragraph (b) or (c) of the definition of risk assessed role in the [Worker Screening Rules]; (c) an individual (who is a registered NDIS provider) whose role is of the kind mentioned in paragraph (b) or (c)". Gist: in these Rules a sole-trader provider is a "worker" only when their role is direct delivery or more than incidental contact; the Worker Screening Rules' definition is wider.

## s 5, certification

s 5(1): an assessment "by conducting: (a) a desk audit ...; and (b) an inspection of the sites, facilities, equipment and services ...; and (c) interviews with relevant persons, including key personnel of the applicant or provider and persons receiving, or to receive, supports or services". s 5(1A): "must be proportionate to: (a) the size of the provider or applicant; and (b) the scale ...; and (c) the scope and complexity". s 5(2): "may be conducted by an appropriate sampling method".

## s 13B, mid-term audit

s 13B(1): applies to a provider registered for a class "for which, under the table in subsection 20(3), the assessment method for the applicable standards is certification". s 13B(2): "The registration of the provider is subject to the condition that the provider must undergo an audit in accordance with this section." s 13B(3): "by an approved quality auditor using certification". s 13B(4): "must commence no later than: (a) 18 months after the beginning of the period for which the provider's registration is in force; or (b) such longer period ... as the Commissioner allows". s 13B(5): assess "(a) the standards in Part 3 of Schedule 1; (b) any standard for which a previous assessment ... identified a need for ... a corrective action plan; (c) any standard specified ... by the Commissioner in a written notice". s 13B(7): does not apply to a partnership or individual whose only certification class is early intervention supports for early childhood, an SDA-only provider, or "a transitioned provider or a transitioned RAC provider". Gist: "a second audit that most certification providers must have" is a fair inline reading; "must" rests on s 13B(2), "most" on s 13B(7).

## s 13BA, change of ownership audit

Where "a significant change in the organisation or governance arrangements of the provider occurs as a result of a change ... in ownership", the registration is subject to a certification audit (s 13BA(2), (3)) within the 3-month window s 13BA(1)(c) describes.

## s 19, purpose

Note 2: "Non-compliance with the NDIS Practice Standards by a registered NDIS provider constitutes a breach of a condition of registration (see paragraph 73F(2)(c) and section 73J of the Act)."

## s 20, class of supports, standards and method

s 20(1): to be registered for a class, an applicant must "be assessed by an approved quality auditor, using the method specified in column 3 of that item, as meeting each standard that: (i) is specified in a Schedule mentioned in column 2 ...; and (ii) applies to the provider; and (b) if the supports are to be provided in circumstances described in subsection 7(2)—be assessed ... as meeting the standards specified in Schedule 4, using certification". s 20(2): to remain registered, "must comply with the standards specified in: (a) column 2 of that item; and (b) if ... subsection 7(2)—Schedule 4". Gist: the standards bind between audits, not only at audit.

## s 23

"if compliance with an applicable standard must be assessed using verification, the requirement is met if compliance with the standard is assessed using certification."

## s 24

"The quality indicators set out in the ... Guidelines 2018 (as existing from time to time) must be taken into account in assessing ... (a) whether an applicant ... meets the NDIS Practice Standards; (b) whether a registered NDIS provider is complying with those standards."

# Study: NDIS (Practice Standards—Worker Screening) Rules 2018

Compilation No. 4, compilation date 31 July 2021, includes amendments up to F2021L01050, registered 12 August 2021; the register's latest version on 16 Oct 2026 (F2021C00788 on the version listing, register id F2018L00887). https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2018L00887/2021-07-31/2021-07-31/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html, authorised text `rev2/dl/ws.txt`. Read 16 Oct 2026. VERIFIED throughout.

## s 4, application

s 4(1): Parts 2 and 4 apply to "(a) a person or entity who is applying to become a registered NDIS provider; and (b) all registered NDIS providers in participating jurisdictions". s 4(2): "Part 3 ... applies to all registered NDIS providers." s 4(3): Part 4 applies "if the circumstances set out in Part 4 apply (see sections 14 and 14A and Part 4)". Gist: s 13 binds applicants as well, through s 9(1) ("as if any reference ... to 'registered NDIS provider' were a reference to 'applicant'"), so the result-page reader who is applying is within reach of the standard, not only "after registration".

## s 5, definitions

"clearance means a decision made under the NDIS worker screening law of a participating jurisdiction: (a) in response to an application for an NDIS worker screening check; (b) having the effect that the person who made the application is cleared to work with people with disability in a risk assessed role; ... being a decision which is current and operative." "exclusion", "interim bar" (a decision "before a decision has been made to issue an exclusion or a clearance ... having the effect that the person is barred"), "suspension" and "closed" are each defined. "NDIS worker screening unit means the person or body which is responsible for conducting NDIS worker screening checks for a State or Territory under its NDIS worker screening law." "risk assessed role means: (a) a key personnel role of a person or an entity; (b) a role for which the normal duties include the direct delivery of specified supports or specified services to a person with disability; or (c) a role for which the normal duties are likely to require more than incidental contact with a person with disability." "worker means any of the following: (a) an individual employed or otherwise engaged by a registered NDIS provider; (b) each of the key personnel of a registered NDIS provider; (c) a partner of a partnership that is a registered NDIS provider; (d) an individual who is a registered NDIS provider." "personnel" are individuals "whose services are made available by a personnel provider". Gist: in these Rules the sole-trader provider is a worker (paragraph (d)) and holds a risk assessed role by definition (paragraph (a) of that definition, through Act s 11A(1)(a)); REQ-027's withdrawal of "You count as a worker too" rests on the Act, which has no such definition, while these Rules say the provider is one.

## s 6, more than incidental contact

"contact includes physical contact, face-to-face contact, oral communication, written communication and electronic communication." s 6(2): normal duties are likely to require more than incidental contact if they include "(a) physically touching a person with disability; or (b) building a rapport with a person with disability as an integral and ordinary part of the performance of those duties; or (c) having contact with multiple people with disability: (i) as part of the direct delivery of a specialist disability support or service; or (ii) in a specialist disability accommodation setting". The two examples (the equipment deliverer, the back-office accountant) are in the text.

## ss 8 to 10

s 8(1): Part 2 is "made for the purposes of subsection 73T(1) of the Act"; note 2: non-compliance "constitutes a breach of a condition of registration". s 9(1): assessed "by verification" as meeting Part 2 and Part 4 "as if any reference in those Parts to 'registered NDIS provider' were a reference to 'applicant'". s 10: certification satisfies verification.

## ss 11 and 12, risk assessed roles and the plan

s 11: a provider "must: (a) assess all roles ... by ... (i) workers; (ii) personnel; and (b) identify each role assessed under paragraph (a) that is a risk assessed role"; note: "A registered NDIS provider who is an individual is a worker". s 12(1): "develop and maintain a written risk management plan for protecting people with disability while any workers or personnel are: (a) engaged in a risk assessed role ...; and (b) in the process of obtaining a clearance"; s 12(2): the plan "must: (a) identify and describe each risk to a person with disability for whom the registered NDIS provider provides supports or services; and (b) explain how each risk will be managed"; s 12(3): review "if: (a) the Commissioner requires ...; or (b) there is a reportable incident involving a worker or any personnel"; s 12(4): "must implement the risk management plan, unless otherwise agreed by the Commissioner".

## s 13, clearance

"Subject to the exceptions contained in this Division, a registered NDIS provider that provides supports or services to a person with disability in a participating jurisdiction: (a) must only allow a worker to engage in a risk assessed role, if the worker has a clearance; and (b) must only allow an individual who is personnel to engage in a risk assessed role, if the registered NDIS provider has: (i) identified to the relevant personnel provider each risk assessed role ...; (ii) entered into an appropriate contract ...; (iii) taken reasonable steps to satisfy itself that the individual has a clearance; and (c) if the registered NDIS provider is an individual—must only engage in a risk assessed role if the registered NDIS provider has a clearance." Gist: D-2723's line rests on (c); the condition is a condition of registration, and through s 9(1) an applicant is assessed against it.

## s 14, exceptions

s 14(1)(a): all four of "(i) the person is in the process of obtaining a clearance; (ii) the person is appropriately supervised by a person with a clearance; (iii) the registered NDIS provider is implementing a risk management plan developed and maintained in accordance with Division 3; (iv) the law of the participating jurisdiction in which the person provides supports or services to a person with disability allows the person to engage in a risk assessed role while that person is in the process of obtaining a clearance". s 14(1)(b): all of "(i) the registered NDIS provider is complying with the transitional arrangements set out in Part 4 that apply in relation to the person in the participating jurisdiction in which supports or services are provided ...; (ii) the person has not been issued with an interim bar ... and is not subject to an exclusion ...; (iii) the person is not subject to a suspension ... and has not had a clearance cancelled". s 14(1)(c): a secondary school student "directly supervised by a worker of the provider who has a clearance". s 14(2)(a) and (b): the same two for "a registered NDIS provider who is an individual", with (2)(a)(ii) "appropriately supervised by a person with a clearance".

## s 14A, "no card, no start" jurisdictions

s 14A(1): paragraphs 14(1)(a) and 14(2)(a) "do not apply to a person if: (a) the person has submitted an application ...; and (b) the law of the participating jurisdiction in which the application is submitted prohibits a person from engaging in a risk assessed role while the person is in the process of obtaining a clearance; and (c) the person does not have an acceptable check that applies in that participating jurisdiction; and (d) a decision has not been made". s 14A(2) keys the acceptable check to the state the participant is in: "(a) ... New South Wales—the person has an acceptable NSW check or an acceptable aged care provider check; (b) ... South Australia—... an acceptable SA check ...; (c) ... Victoria—the person is allowed to engage in a risk assessed role because of subsection 25(3) or an acceptable aged care check; (d) ... Queensland—... subsection 26(3) ...; (e) ... Tasmania—... subsection 27(3) ...; (f) ... Australian Capital Territory—... subsection 28(3) ...; (g) ... Northern Territory—... subsection 29(3) ...; (h) ... Western Australia—the person has an acceptable WA check or an acceptable aged care check". Gist: the Rules themselves defer to state law twice (s 14(1)(a)(iv) and s 14A(1)(b)); the Rules never purport to override a state prohibition.

## s 15, in the process of obtaining a clearance

s 15(1): "during the period starting on the day on which the person submits a complete application for a clearance to the relevant NDIS worker screening unit, and ending on the day on which a clearance or exclusion is made". s 15(2): never "(a) if ... on the most recent prior occasion the person was subject to an exclusion; (b) if ... the clearance was revoked; (c) on any day after the application ... is withdrawn ...; (d) on any day after the application ... is closed; (e) on any day when an interim bar is in force ...; (f) at any time the person is not being supervised in accordance with the requirements of section 14 ...; or (g) at any time a risk management plan required under this instrument is not being implemented". s 15(3): a complete application only if "(a) that unit has issued a notice in writing ... confirming that the application has been made; and (aa) a registered NDIS provider has confirmed to that unit that: (i) the person is, or intends to be, a worker; or (ii) if the registered NDIS provider is an individual—the person is the registered NDIS provider; and (b) ... the registered NDIS provider ... has seen that notice; and (c) ... has made a record of the worker screening application number on that notice as required by section 18". s 15(4): confirmation "in accordance with any applicable requirements of the NDIS worker screening law of the participating jurisdiction in which the application has been submitted".

## Part 3, records (ss 16 to 21)

s 16(1): "made for the purposes of section 73Q of the Act"; note: compliance "is a condition of registration (see paragraph 73F(2)(d) ...), breach of which is a civil penalty provision". s 17(1): "a written list of all roles with the registered NDIS provider which are risk assessed roles that are engaged in by a person"; note: "irrespective of whether the person who engages in the role is a worker, is personnel or is a registered NDIS provider who is an individual"; s 17(2)(b): updated "within 20 business days"; s 17(3): the list "must include: (a) the title or other organisational identifier for the role; (b) which paragraph or paragraphs of the definition of risk assessed role applies ...; (c) a description of the role; (d) the date ...; (e) the name and title of the person who assessed the role". s 18(1): "a written list of: (a) each worker who engages in a risk assessed role; and (b) the information mentioned in subsection (3)"; s 18(2): "keep the written list up-to-date"; s 18(3): "(a) the worker's full name, date of birth and address; (b) the risk assessed role or roles ...; (c) if ... without a clearance: (i) the exception under section 14 ...; (ii) the start and end date ...; (iii) ... the name of the person who supervises the worker; (d) if ... with a clearance: (i) the worker's NDIS worker screening check application number; and (ii) the worker's NDIS worker screening check number; and (iii) the ... outcome and any expiry date ...; (iv) whether the worker's clearance is subject to a decision to suspend or revoke ...; and (v) the nature of any decision". s 18(4): copies of records about "an interim bar; (b) a suspension; (c) an exclusion; or (d) action taken by the provider". s 18(5): copies about "allegations of any misconduct against any worker with an NDIS worker screening check clearance" and the action taken. s 19: the personnel records. s 20: the s 18(1) list "in a form which would allow an auditor to determine which workers were engaged in risk assessed roles with the provider on any given day in the seven years prior to the day the auditor examines the list". s 21: "A record subject to this Part must be kept for 7 years from the date the record is made." Gist: the check number and the outcome are records the provider must keep and show an auditor in a form the auditor can examine (s 20 names "an auditor" as the reader); the Rules themselves contemplate the auditor seeing the list with the check numbers in it.

## Part 4, transitional and special arrangements (ss 22 to 30)

s 22(1): Part 4 is "made for the purposes of subsection 73T(1) of the Act and sections 14 and 14A". s 23 (NSW): "acceptable NSW check" at (5) requires a criminal record check obtained "on or before 30 June 2018" and "no more than 4 years before that time"; (6) during the transition period and "no more than 2 years before that time"; (7) a NSW working with children check "issued ... no later than the last day of the transition period"; (10): the check "expires at the end of the last day that it meets the requirements"; (12): the NSW transition period ends "31 January 2021" or earlier on a s 30 notice. s 24 (SA): (6) a finding "not more than 3 years since"; (6A) likewise; (6B) a working with children determination "during the transition period" and "not more than 5 years since the determination"; (8) expiry at the end of the last day the requirement is met; (10) the SA transition period ends 31 January 2021 or on notice. s 25 (Victoria): (3) during the transition period, the provider "compliant with the safety screening requirements in relation to the person in accordance with the safety screening policy issued by the Victorian Department of Health and Human Services and as in force from time to time" and "the person's safety screening is current"; (5) after the end of the transition period, "(b) before the end of the transition period, the provider was compliant with the safety screening requirements ...; and (c) the person's safety screening is current in accordance with the requirements; and (d) any of the following apply: (i) the person has a current working with children check ... issued to the person before the end of the transition period; (ii) less than 12 months have elapsed since the end of the transition period; (iii) 12 months or more have elapsed since the end of the transition period and the person is in the process of obtaining a clearance; and (e) a notice (if any) ... has not come into effect"; (8) the Victorian transition period "(a) starting on 1 July 2019; and (b) ending on the earlier of: (i) 31 January 2021; and (ii) the day notice is given under section 30". s 26 (Queensland): (4) after the transition period, only "before the transition time for the person", which (5) ties to the expiry of the notice or certificate held at the end of the transition period or the decision on the pending application. s 27 (Tasmania): (6) transition time "the earlier of: (a) 3 years after the person was registered ...; and (b) the expiry of the registration". s 28 (ACT): (6) likewise 3 years or expiry. s 29 (NT): (6) "the earlier of: (a) 2 years after the person was given a clearance notice ...; and (b) the expiry"; (9) the NT transition period ends 30 June 2021. s 29A (WA): (5) and (6) "at a time (the relevant time) before 1 February 2023"; (8) the WA transition period "(a) starting on 1 December 2020; and (b) ending on the earlier of: (i) 31 January 2023; and (ii) the day notice is given". s 29B (residential aged care): (5)(c) and (6)(d) "the police certificate is issued before 1 February 2021"; (10): "This section ceases to have effect at the end of 1 February 2024." s 30: the Minister "may, by notifiable instrument, give notice that the NDIS worker screening unit for a State or Territory is operational".

Gist for the Victorian pair: s 25(5)(d)(iii) has no date of its own, but it sits inside (5), which also needs (5)(b), compliance "before the end of the transition period" with a Victorian departmental policy "as in force from time to time", and (5)(c), a screening that "is current in accordance with the requirements". Whether that policy still exists in force, and whether any screening under it can still be "current" five years on, are facts outside the Rules' text. And s 14(1)(b)(i) only ever applied Part 4 "in the participating jurisdiction in which supports or services are provided"; it did not say that Part 4 displaces the law of that jurisdiction. The Rules read as a whole defer to state law (s 14(1)(a)(iv), s 14A(1)(b)); the Act makes state law a condition of registration (s 73F(2)(a)).

# Study: NDIS (Incident Management and Reportable Incidents) Rules 2018

As made, F2018L00633, registered 17 May 2018; no compilation existed on the register on 16 Oct 2026 (the API's latest version is compilation 0, start 18 May 2018). https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2018L00633/2018-05-18/2018-05-18/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html. Read 16 Oct 2026. VERIFIED for ss 16 to 21.

## s 16, what is and is not a reportable incident

s 16(1) note 2: "Subsection 73Z(5) of the Act allows this instrument to provide that specified acts, omissions or events are, or are not, reportable incidents. This instrument can override subsection 73Z(4) of the Act in this regard." s 16(2): an act under s 73Z(4)(d) "is not a reportable incident if: (a) the act is unlawful physical contact ...; and (b) the contact with, and impact on, the person with disability is negligible". s 16(3): "Despite paragraph 73Z(4)(f) of the Act, the use of a restrictive practice ... where the use is in accordance with an authorisation (however described) of a State or Territory is a reportable incident if the use is not in accordance with a behaviour support plan for the person with disability." s 16(4): "Despite paragraph 73Z(4)(f) of the Act, the use of a restrictive practice ... where the use is not in accordance with an authorisation ... is not a reportable incident if: (a) the use is in accordance with a behaviour support plan ...; and (b) the State or Territory in which the restrictive practice is used does not have authorisation process". Gist: REQ-027's Domain rules say the Rules "add the use of a restrictive practice not in accordance with a behaviour support plan"; that is s 16(3), and s 16(4) also removes a case. The glossary `more`'s "a restrictive practice used without authorisation" is the Act's limb and omits both adjustments; the inline meaning "an incident the law and the Rules list" is right because both instruments make the list.

## s 17

"A reference in this Part to a reportable incident that has occurred includes a reference to a reportable incident that is alleged to have occurred."

## ss 18 and 19, who must act

s 18: key personnel and the s 10(1)(c) person "must take all reasonable steps to ensure that reportable incidents ... are notified to the Commissioner". s 19: a person "employed or otherwise engaged by a registered NDIS provider" who becomes aware "must notify ... as soon as possible" a member of key personnel, a supervisor or manager, or the s 10(1)(c) person.

## s 20, the 24-hour route

s 20(1)(b): death, serious injury, abuse or neglect, unlawful sexual or physical contact or assault, sexual misconduct. s 20(2): "must notify the Commissioner of the following information within 24 hours: (a) the name and contact details of the registered NDIS provider; (b) a description of the reportable incident; ... (e) the name and contact details of the person making the notification; ... (g) the names and contact details of the persons involved". s 20(3): where "insufficient information is available", give "(a) ... paragraphs (2)(a) to (e) within the 24 hour period; and (b) ... the remaining information ... within 5 business days". s 20(4): within 5 business days, "(a) the names and contact details of any witnesses ...; (b) any further actions proposed". s 20(5): the 24-hour notification "may be given by telephone or in writing". s 20(7): the Commissioner "must acknowledge its receipt within 24 hours". Gist: the register must never be the place the s 20(2)(g) names go, which the product's no-participant-data rule already prevents; the names go to the Commission's form, not to Readyline.

## s 21, the 5-business-day route

s 21(1): applies where the incident "is not of a kind covered by paragraph 20(1)(b)", which after s 16 is the restrictive-practice limb. s 21(2): "(a) be given in writing; and (b) be given within 5 business days after the provider became aware that the reportable incident occurred". Gist: the primer's "within 24 hours if it caused harm" is attributed to a Commission page and is not in ss 20 or 21 of the Rules as made; UNVERIFIED here.

## Records

s 25(1): a provider that becomes aware of a reportable incident "must keep a record of the incident"; s 25(2): "The record must be kept for 7 years from the day that notification of the reportable incident is given under subsection 20(2), paragraph 21(2)(b) or subsection 23(2)". VERIFIED.

# Study: NDIS (Code of Conduct) Rules 2018

Compilation No. 1, F2024C00048, compilation date 19 December 2023, includes F2023L01703, registered 12 January 2024. https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2018L00629/2023-12-19/2023-12-19/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html. Read 16 Oct 2026. VERIFIED for ss 5 and 6.

## s 5, who is covered

s 5(1): "This Part is made for the purposes of section 73V of the Act." s 5(2): "Both of the following are Code-covered persons: (a) NDIS providers; (b) persons employed or otherwise engaged by NDIS providers or members of the key personnel of NDIS providers." s 5(3): "Code-covered persons must comply with the NDIS Code of Conduct set out in section 6"; note: "Compliance with the NDIS Code of Conduct is a civil penalty provision (see section 73V of the Act)." Gist: the Code binds unregistered and registered providers alike and their workers; it is the one NDIS obligation a provider has before registration, which matters for what Readyline says to a not-yet-registered reader about "must report".

## s 6, the Code

s 6(1): "In providing supports or services to people with disability, a Code-covered person must: (a) act with respect for individual rights to freedom of expression, self-determination and decision-making ...; (b) respect the privacy of people with disability; (c) provide supports and services in a safe and competent manner, with care and skill; (d) act with integrity, honesty and transparency; (e) promptly take steps to raise and act on concerns about matters that may impact the quality and safety of supports and services ...; (f) take all reasonable steps to prevent and respond to all forms of violence against, and exploitation, neglect and abuse of, people with disability; and (g) take all reasonable steps to prevent and respond to sexual misconduct." s 6(2): a Code-covered person "must not, without a reasonable justification: (a) represent a price for the supply of goods for a participant that is higher than the price represented ... for a person who is not a participant; or (b) charge a price ... higher". Gist: (b) is the provision CLAUDE.md 3a's "privacy provision of the NDIS Code of Conduct" refers to when it speaks of participant information in public AI tools; the Code says "respect the privacy", not "de-identify", so the product's de-identification rule is Readyline's own standard and the Commission's expectation, not the Code's words.

# Study: the state and territory worker screening Acts

Read 16 Oct 2026. Each state: the instrument and version, what was reached, the prohibition on working without a clearance, any pending-application allowance, and the secrecy or confidentiality provision with who it binds. The two questions these answer for REQ-024 SR-5 and D-2446 are whether a provider may pass a worker's check number and decision code to an approved quality auditor, and whether a Commonwealth Part 4 arrangement can stand where the state Act prohibits the work.

## Victoria, Worker Screening Act 2020 (Vic), No. 34 of 2020, version 017 (30 September 2026). VERIFIED for ss 35, 36, 118, 119, 120, 130 and the table of provisions.

Structure: Chapter 6 is "Offences" (Part 6.1 NDIS clearances ss 118 to 120; Part 6.3 General, s 130 "Confidentiality of information"); Chapter 7 is "Information sharing" (ss 131 to 143); Chapter 8 "General" holds Part 8.1 (offences by bodies corporate), Part 8.2 (miscellaneous) and Part 8.3 (regulations). REQ-024 SR-5's citation "Worker Screening Act 2020 (Vic) Part 8" therefore points at the wrong Part: Part 8.1 to 8.3 hold no secrecy provision; the confidentiality section is s 130 and the sharing powers are Chapter 7.

s 118(1): "A person who does not hold an NDIS clearance or an interstate NDIS clearance must not engage in work in a risk assessed role, except in circumstances referred to in section 119 or 120. Penalty: Level 7 imprisonment (2 years maximum) or a level 7 fine (240 penalty units maximum) or both." s 118(2): no offence if the person "did not know that the work was in a risk assessed role" or did not know and "was not reckless as to whether" they held a clearance.

s 119(1): a person without a clearance "may engage in work in a risk assessed role if— (a) the person has applied in accordance with section 35 for a new NDIS check to be carried out and— (i) the application has not been finally decided or withdrawn; and (ii) the person has not been given an interim NDIS exclusion ...; and (b) the person— (i) has not at any time been given an NDIS exclusion ...; (ii) is not subject to a relevant obligation or order; and (iii) is not charged with or has not been convicted ... of an NDIS category A offence or an NDIS category B offence". s 35: "A person who holds an NDIS clearance or an interstate NDIS clearance may apply for the carrying out of an NDIS check and a new NDIS clearance at any time within 3 months before the expiry". s 119(2): a person who "has applied for an interstate NDIS clearance under a corresponding NDIS worker screening law" may work if "permitted under that corresponding NDIS worker screening law ... pending determination". s 120: the secondary school student, with the note that "Rule 14(c) of the NDIS Worker Screening Rules provides" for the exemption. Gist: Victoria's only pending-application allowance for a Victorian application is a renewal by an existing holder (s 35); a first-time applicant in Victoria commits an offence by working, so "no clearance, no start" is right for a first application and wrong for a renewal lodged within the three months. Nothing in s 118 or s 119 refers to Part 4 of the Commonwealth Rules; a provider relying on s 25(5)(d)(iii) of those Rules for a first-time applicant would have the worker commit the s 118 offence, and would itself be in breach of the s 73F(2)(a) condition.

s 130(1): "A person must not give to any other person, whether directly or indirectly, any information acquired by the person in the performance of functions or exercise of powers under this Act. Penalty: Level 9 fine (60 penalty units maximum)." s 130(3) exceptions include "(b) with the written authority of the person to whom the information relates", "(g) to an Australian legal practitioner for the purpose of obtaining legal advice", "(h) as required or permitted by or under this Act or any other law". Gist: the duty attaches to information "acquired ... in the performance of functions or exercise of powers under this Act"; a provider that learns a worker's clearance number from the worker or from the Commission's database is not performing a function under the Victorian Act, so s 130 does not on its face reach a provider showing that number to an auditor; whether the Victorian Regulator's notices to an employer (Chapter 7) carry a condition is UNVERIFIED (Chapter 7 read by heading only).

## Queensland, Disability Services Act 2006 (Qld), current reprint. VERIFIED for ss 40 to 44, 53, 54, 138ZLB, 138ZLC, 228, Schedule 8 "screening information".

s 53(1): "A registered NDIS provider must not engage, or continue to engage, a person to carry out risk-assessed NDIS work unless the person holds an NDIS clearance or interstate NDIS clearance", maximum "200 penalty units or 2 years imprisonment" with an aggravating circumstance, "100 penalty units" otherwise; s 53(3) exempts a subcontractor where "the provider has complied with the NDIS (Worker Screening) Practice Standards". s 54(1): "A person must not start, or continue, an engagement to carry out risk-assessed NDIS work for a registered NDIS provider unless the person holds an NDIS clearance or interstate NDIS clearance", maximum "500 penalty units or 5 years imprisonment" with aggravation; s 54(2): "A person who is a registered NDIS provider must not carry out risk-assessed NDIS work as an NDIS sole trader unless the person holds an NDIS clearance or interstate NDIS clearance. Maximum penalty—500 penalty units or 5 years imprisonment." s 43: the secondary school student on work experience under direct supervision. Gist: Queensland is "no clearance, no start" in the Act's own words, for workers and for the sole trader; no pending-application allowance was found in Part 5 Division 1 or ss 53 to 56 (the Division was not read line by line beyond those sections, so an allowance elsewhere is UNVERIFIED).

s 138ZLB(1): applies to "a person who— (a) is or has been— (i) a Minister or a member of the Minister's staff; or (ii) a public service employee employed in the department; and (b) in that capacity, was given, or given access to, screening information"; (2): "must not use the screening information, or disclose or give access to the screening information to anyone else", maximum 100 penalty units; (3)(f) excepts a disclosure that "happens with the consent of the person to whom the screening information relates". Schedule 8: "screening information— (a) means confidential information obtained for the screening of a person under part 5". s 228(2): a person who "gains confidential information through involvement in this Act's administration ... must not disclose the information", and s 228(3) lists who that is (the chief executive, departmental employees, contractors to the department, researchers, committee members). s 138ZLC: the chief executive "may disclose screening information to the NDIS commissioner". Gist: the Queensland secrecy provisions bind Ministers, the department and its contractors, not a registered provider; a provider passing a check number to an auditor is outside them on their face.

## Western Australia, National Disability Insurance Scheme (Worker Screening) Act 2020 (WA), consolidation 00-i0-01 (25 September 2025). VERIFIED for ss 68, 69, 71, 72 and the contents.

Structure: Part 6 "General" holds s 68 (the offence) and s 71 (use or disclosure); Part 7 is "Consequential amendments to other Acts". REQ-024 SR-5's "Part 7" is the wrong Part.

s 68(1): "A person must not start or continue to be employed or otherwise engaged by a registered NDIS provider in NDIS work which involves (wholly or in part) a risk assessed role unless— (a) the person is the holder of an NDIS worker check clearance certificate and the certificate is not suspended; or (b) the person has applied for an NDIS worker check clearance, the application is pending, and the person is not subject to an interim bar. Penalty for this subsection: imprisonment for 5 years and a fine of $60 000." s 68(2): the same for a person "subject to an NDIS worker check exclusion certificate". Gist: WA allows work while an application is pending (s 68(1)(b)); the Commonwealth s 14(1)(a) exception is live in WA, subject to its own four limbs.

s 71: "A person who is or has been engaged in the performance of functions under this Act must not, directly or indirectly, disclose or make use of information obtained in the course of performing those functions except— (a) for the purpose of ... performing functions under this Act; ... (d) where the disclosure is for the purpose of providing reports to the NDIS Commission, or is otherwise connected with the operation of the National Disability Insurance Scheme; ... (h) with the written consent of the Minister or the person to whom the information relates. Penalty: imprisonment for 2 years and a fine of $24 000." s 72(2) and (3): information disclosed to or by the CEO for the Act's purposes "may be disclosed despite any other enactment, law or agreement that prohibits or restricts its disclosure". Gist: s 71 binds persons performing functions under the Act; a provider showing a certificate number to an auditor is not within it on its face, and (d) would cover a disclosure "connected with the operation of the National Disability Insurance Scheme" even for one who was.

## Tasmania, Registration to Work with Vulnerable People Act 2013 (Tas), authorised version 1 December 2025. VERIFIED for ss 16, 16A, 18 (opening), 54.

## Across the states, the two answers as far as the texts reach

On secrecy (SR-5, D-2446): in Victoria (s 130), Queensland (ss 138ZLB, 228), Western Australia (s 71), Tasmania (s 54) and the ACT (s 65) the secrecy provision binds the Regulator, the department and persons performing functions under the Act, and in each the information protected is information acquired in performing those functions. None of the five, on its face, binds a registered provider in respect of a clearance number or an outcome it learned from the worker or the Commonwealth database, and each excepts disclosure with the person's consent. The Commonwealth Rules s 18(3)(d) require the provider to record the check number and outcome and s 20 requires the list to be kept in a form "which would allow an auditor to determine" who was engaged on any day, so the Rules contemplate the auditor reading it. What remains for the practising lawyer: NSW and the NT, unread; whether a unit's outcome notice to an employer carries a use condition under Chapter 7 (Vic) or the equivalent; and the Privacy Act question for the provider (an exclusion or interim-bar code as information about a "criminal record", s 6 "sensitive information", if the provider is an APP entity under s 6D(4)(b) and s 6FB(3)(b)).

On Victoria's s 25(5)(d)(iii) (SR-5 second question): the Victorian Act's s 118 prohibits the work and s 119 allows it only for a renewal applicant under s 35; the Act s 73F(2)(a) makes compliance with "a law of the State or Territory in which the person or entity operates" a condition of registration; the Rules' own s 14(1)(a)(iv) and s 14A(1)(b) defer to the state's law. The role's read is that a Commonwealth Part 4 arrangement cannot be relied on where the state Act prohibits the work, and that the first Victorian question (whether (5)(b) and (c) can still be met) is moot for a first-time applicant and depends on facts outside the Rules (whether the DHHS "safety screening policy ... as in force from time to time" still exists) for anyone else. A practising lawyer is still needed because the construction of "allows" in s 14(1)(a)(iv) and of "complying with the transitional arrangements" in s 14(1)(b)(i) against a state offence is a question of inconsistency the role cannot settle from the words, and because the Victorian policy's current status is a fact not in any text read.

# Study: Trade Marks Act 1995 (Cth)

Compilation No. 48, C2026C00421, 19 September 2026, includes Act No. 87 of 2026. https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A04969/2026-09-19/2026-09-19/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html. Read 16 Oct 2026. VERIFIED for ss 120(1), (2) and 122(1), (2).

## s 120, infringement

s 120(1): "A person infringes a registered trade mark if the person uses as a trade mark a sign that is substantially identical with, or deceptively similar to, the trade mark in relation to goods or services in respect of which the trade mark is registered." s 120(2): the same for goods or services "of the same description" or "closely related", but "the person is not taken to have infringed the trade mark if the person establishes that using the sign as the person did is not likely to deceive or cause confusion". Gist: infringement needs use "as a trade mark", that is, as a badge of origin for Readyline's own services; writing "NDIS Worker Screening Check" to name the check the Act defines (NDIS Act s 9, "NDIS worker screening check") is use to name a thing, not to brand Readyline.

## s 122, when not infringed

s 122(1): "In spite of section 120, a person does not infringe a registered trade mark when: ... (b) the person uses a sign in good faith to indicate: (i) the kind, quality, quantity, intended purpose, value, geographical origin, or some other characteristic, of goods or services; ... (c) the person uses the trade mark in good faith to indicate the intended purpose of goods ... or services; (d) the person uses the trade mark for the purposes of comparative advertising". Gist: "documents aligned to the NDIS Practice Standards" and "prepare for NDIS registration" describe the intended purpose and a characteristic of Readyline's services; the brand itself carries no "NDIS" (CLAUDE.md 4.1), the pages say "NDIS" is the NDIA's mark used "only to describe what the service relates to" (`product/legal/terms.md:14`), and the footer says the service is not affiliated (`src/lib/brand.ts`, `INDEPENDENCE_STATEMENT`). What the role cannot settle: the registration particulars of the NDIA's marks (classes, any endorsements), which are on IP Australia's register and were not read (UNVERIFIED), and the NDIA's own trade mark guidelines page (CLAUDE.md section 9, not re-read on the day).
