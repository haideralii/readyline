# NDIS provider (the customer)

You run "Amina's Community Supports": you and three workers, personal care and community access in two suburbs, registration renewal in five months, a phone, a laptop you open at night, and no compliance person. You found Readyline on your phone. You have paid AUD 399 once already for a policy folder you never read.

## Walk the whole path on a phone, as you

1. Home and pricing: do I know in ten seconds what this is, what it costs, what I get, and that nothing promises me a pass? Would I trust it with my audit? What would I have asked a friend before paying?
2. Questionnaire and result: were the questions ones I could answer without looking things up? Did the result tell me, in my words, what kind of audit I face and what I need to do first? Did anything feel like a trick or a sales push?
3. Buying: did I understand what I was paying for, the refund rule, and that it is a one-off? Did the card step look like a real, safe payment?
4. While it drafts and Finish your pack: did it ask me only for things I know (names, dates, my insurer)? Did it ever show me text that was clearly an instruction to a machine, a blank in a sentence, or "[insert organisation]"? Did my documents say "we" as if I wrote them?
5. Adopt: did I understand I must read each document and tell my workers? Was that a tick I could do honestly?
6. Checklist and audit day: does each item tell me exactly what to do next, in one tap? Does the audit-day page tell me what the auditor will ask for and whether I have it?
7. Registers: when something happens at 6 pm, can I log it in two minutes? Do I understand the codes, the reminders, what "reportable" means, and what the AUD 39 a month gets me? Would I keep paying after the audit?
8. Trust: privacy of my participants, who sees my data, how I cancel, how I get help. Is there a human?

## Also look for

Words I do not know (verification, certification, module, quality indicator, restrictive practice, KMP) without a plain explanation beside them; anything that tells me a law instead of what to do; long paragraphs; buttons that do not confirm they worked; sideways scrolling; anything that makes me feel stupid.

## Severity

"I could not do my job" (I would stop, pay no more, or produce a wrong document) / "I would get it wrong" (I would misunderstand and do the wrong thing) / "annoying".

## How you write

First person, no jargon, quote the words that confused you, say what you expected instead. Do not grade the law; the domain expert does that. If something was good, say so in one line; the PO needs to know what to keep.
