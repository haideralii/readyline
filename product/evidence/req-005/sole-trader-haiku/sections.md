  > Sunrise Supports is a sole trader. The owner is the only person who delivers supports, so the steps below are written for one person. If we later engage workers, this section will be updated before they start.
  > Deliver household task supports (support type 0120) to each participant according to their agreed support plan and any written instructions in the plan.
  > Check at the start of each visit that the participant is safe and that the support environment is safe to work in.
  > If something goes wrong during a visit, stop the task if it is safe to do so and make sure the participant is safe first.
  > As soon as the participant is safe, record what happened in the incident register (see section 9 Incident register). Do this on the day the incident occurs or becomes known.
  > Decide whether the incident is a reportable incident using section 7 Reportable incidents. If it may be reportable, act on it without delay.
  > Contact the participant, and their nominee or family where the participant has agreed, to explain what happened and what we are doing about it.
  > Work through the response steps in section 8 Incident procedure, including any follow-up action needed to prevent a repeat.
  > Review the incident register at the end of each week to check that every entry has an outcome, a follow-up action and a date for closing it.

  > This section sets out what Sunrise Supports does, step by step, when an incident happens. As a sole trader working alone, the owner carries out every step below. Reportable incidents and their timeframes are set out in section 7 Reportable incidents, and the register is kept as described in section 9 Incident register.
  > Make sure the participant is safe and gets any first aid or medical help they need. Call emergency services on 000 if there is immediate danger.
  > Stop any risk that is still happening, for example by removing a hazard or leaving an unsafe situation.
  > Check whether the incident is a reportable incident under section 7 Reportable incidents. If it may be, note the time you became aware of it, because the reporting clock starts then.
  > Tell the participant, and their nominee or family member if the participant wants that, what happened and what we are doing about it, in plain language.
  > Write down what happened as soon as possible, while it is fresh. Record the date, time, place, people involved, what was seen or done, and any action taken. Keep to facts and avoid guessing about cause.
  > Make a report to the Commission within the timeframe in section 7 Reportable incidents if the incident is reportable. Keep a copy of what was sent.
  > Enter the incident in the incident register in section 9 Incident register on the same day, using the next incident number.
  > Review what happened and why. Decide what will prevent it happening again, such as a change to how a support is delivered, a risk assessment update, or a change to a participant's support plan, and record that decision.
  > Carry out each follow-up action, write down when it was done, and check that it has worked.
  > Review the incident record at the end of each month to make sure every incident has a finished outcome, and note any trends in the register.
  > Step | What the owner does | When
  > Immediate response | Makes the participant safe, gets help and stops the risk | Straight away
  > Report decision | Checks whether section 7 Reportable incidents applies and notes the time of awareness | Straight away
  > Record | Writes the incident record and adds it to the register | Same day
  > Report to the Commission | Submits the report if the incident is reportable | Within the timeframe in section 7 Reportable incidents
  > Review and follow up | Finds the cause, sets actions and checks they work | Within a timeframe the owner sets and records
