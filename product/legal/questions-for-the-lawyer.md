## 1. Business structure and the general-information disclaimer

1.1 Readyline trades as a sole trader; does the ACL reach its pages at all, and in what capacity does it contract? Read: yes. [ACL] applies "as a law of the Commonwealth to the conduct of corporations" (CCA s 131(1), VERIFIED) and, for a person who is not a corporation, to conduct that "involves the use of postal, telegraphic or telephonic services" (CCA s 6(3), VERIFIED, for ss 18 and 29) and to "contracts for or relating to ... the use of postal, telegraphic or telephonic services" (CCA s 6(3A), VERIFIED, for the unfair terms Part); a website and email are such services, and the NSW application law (Fair Trading Act 1987 (NSW), UNVERIFIED, not reached) catches the rest. Severity: for a practising lawyer, low. Text change: none; `terms.md:12` ("run by a sole trader in Sydney") and the `{{business-line}}` token already name the party once the ABN exists. Why still the lawyer: to confirm the state application law for the consumer guarantees route and whether the terms should name the trading entity's ABN as the contracting party rather than the product name.

1.2 Is "Readyline provides general information and document templates for your own review. It is not legal advice" (`src/lib/brand.ts:16-19`, `terms.md:18`) an adequate disclaimer, and what does it not do? Read: it is an accurate description, not a cure. [ACL] s 18 ("must not, in trade or commerce, engage in conduct that is misleading or deceptive", VERIFIED) cannot be excluded by a term, and s 64(1) voids a term "to the extent that the term purports to exclude, restrict or modify ... any liability of a person for a failure to comply with a guarantee" (VERIFIED). What the sentence does do is go to reliance under s 61(3) ("unreasonable for the consumer to rely on, the skill or judgment of the supplier", VERIFIED) and to the reader's impression under s 18; it does nothing for a sentence elsewhere that is itself misleading. Severity: unclear, in one place: `terms.md:18` reads "speak to a lawyer or a qualified consultant", which puts a consultant beside a lawyer for "advice about your specific situation" while CLAUDE.md 4.2 says the product must not advise on disputes, employment or contracts. Text change: drop "or a qualified consultant" from the terms' sentence, so the referral for legal questions is to a lawyer alone. Why still the lawyer: whether the generated documents, which carry no statement (CLAUDE.md 4.2, D-058), need a cover sentence that the documents are the provider's own and were not reviewed by a lawyer, is a judgement about the provider's reliance the role cannot make.

1.3 Does anything on the pages amount to unqualified legal practice? Read: not on the words read. The Legal Profession Uniform Law (NSW) s 10 (engaging in legal practice when not entitled) was not reached (UNVERIFIED; CLAUDE.md section 9 cites the Law Society's fact sheet). The pages sell templates and information and say so. Severity: for a practising lawyer. Text change: none. Why still the lawyer: the line between a tailored policy drafted from answers and "legal advice" under the Uniform Law is a question of that Law's definitions, not of the ACL.

## 2. Refunds and the consumer guarantees

2.1 Is a provider that buys the pack for its business a "consumer", so that the guarantees apply? Read: yes. [ACL] s 3(3): a person acquires services as a consumer "if, and only if: (a) the amount paid or payable for the services ... did not exceed ... (ii) if a greater amount is prescribed ... that greater amount" (VERIFIED), and [CCR] reg 77A prescribes "$100,000" (VERIFIED). A $349 to $499 pack and a $29 to $49 month are under it whatever the buyer's purpose. Severity: no finding. Text change: none.

2.2 Is the pack goods or services, and which guarantees attach? Read: services, with the documents as "any product resulting from the services". [ACL] s 60 ("rendered with due care and skill"), s 61(1) (where the consumer "makes known ... any particular purpose", the services "and any product resulting from the services, will be reasonably fit for that purpose"), s 61(2) (the "result that the consumer wishes the services to achieve"), s 62 (reasonable time), all VERIFIED; "goods includes ... (e) computer software" (s 2, VERIFIED) does not naturally cover a `.docx` policy. The questionnaire makes the purpose known (a named audit type), so s 61 is live. Severity: for a practising lawyer. Text change: none. Why still the lawyer: whether an auditor's non-conformance against a generated policy could be argued as a s 61(2) "result" failure, and how the general-information statement bears on s 61(3), is the central exposure of the product and a judgement call.

2.3 Does the refund page limit the buyer's rights, or read as if it does? Read: it does not limit them, because `refunds.md:36` says "Nothing on this page limits your rights under the Australian Consumer Law", and it must not read as the ceiling: [ACL] s 29(1)(m) prohibits a representation "concerning the existence, exclusion or effect of any condition, warranty, guarantee, right or remedy" (VERIFIED). The page's two promises (14 days undownloaded; after download, where the documents "do not match the answers you gave" or "are missing something you were told they would include") sit above the statutory floor, where s 267(2) lets the consumer "require the supplier to remedy the failure within a reasonable time" and s 267(3) lets them "terminate the contract" for a major failure (VERIFIED), with s 269(3) a refund "to the extent that the consumer has not already consumed the services" (VERIFIED). Severity: unclear, in one place: the heading "Our promise" and "we decide in your favour" (`refunds.md:12`, `:18`) are future-matter representations under s 4 (VERIFIED) and need a kept record of refunds decided, which REQ-007's purchase records give. Text change: none to the words; keep a dated log of refund decisions so s 4(2)'s "evidence ... to the contrary" exists.

2.4 Does "You can have a full refund within 14 days of buying the pack if you have not downloaded any of the documents" create a condition the ACL does not allow? Read: no, as a voluntary promise; the downloaded case is still governed by the guarantees, which the page says. Severity: no finding. Text change: none.

2.5 Does `terms.md:50` "our liability to you is limited to the amount you paid Readyline in the 12 months before the claim" survive? Read: only outside the guarantees. [ACL] s 64A(2) lets a services supplier limit guarantee liability only "to: (a) the supplying of the services again; or (b) the payment of the cost of having the services supplied again" and only for services not "of a kind ordinarily acquired for personal, domestic or household use" (VERIFIED); a money cap is neither form, so to the extent it limits guarantee liability it is void under s 64(1) (VERIFIED), and the terms' "Where the law allows" saves the rest. A cap is also s 25(k)'s example of a term "that limits ... one party's right to sue" (VERIFIED) in a standard form small business contract (s 23(4)(b)(i), fewer than 100 employees, VERIFIED; s 27(1) presumption, VERIFIED). Severity: unclear. Text change: say in the same paragraph that for a failure of a consumer guarantee the remedies are those the ACL gives, and keep the cap for other claims; the lawyer settles whether a s 64A(2) re-supply limitation is wanted instead. Why still the lawyer: whether a document pack is "of a kind ordinarily acquired for personal, domestic or household use" (which would take s 64A(2) off the table) and whether the cap is "reasonably necessary" under s 24(1)(b).

2.6 Is the sentence "We are not liable for loss that comes from documents you did not review or adopt, or from information you entered that was not true" (`terms.md:50`) sound? Read: as a statement of causation it tracks s 267(1)(c) (no action where the failure "occurred only because of ... a representation made by, any person other than the supplier", VERIFIED, for guarantees other than s 60); as an exclusion it is subject to s 64. Severity: unclear. Text change: none to the words; the lawyer confirms it is read as causation, not exclusion.

## 3. Subscription terms

3.1 Pausing: `terms.md:30` "If a payment fails, access pauses until it is paid; nothing extra is charged" and `refunds.md:24` "If a payment fails, your access pauses; nothing is added to what you owe". Read: consistent with CLAUDE.md 5 and with [ACL] s 25(a) (a term "permitting ... one party (but not another party) to avoid or limit performance", VERIFIED) because the pause is tied to the customer's own non-payment and nothing accrues; "what you owe" is the open point, since the page never says what is owed after a failed payment. Severity: unclear. Text change: say whether a failed period is owed at all, or whether access simply resumes from the next successful payment, in one sentence on both pages.

3.2 Cancelling: "You can cancel at any time. You keep access until the end of the period you have paid for" (`refunds.md:24`, `terms.md:30`). Read: sound; it is the customer's right, and the easy cancellation CLAUDE.md 5 requires is what keeps the unilateral-variation term (next item) from being one-sided. Severity: no finding. Text change: none.

3.3 "We may change features, prices for future purchases, and these terms. We give at least 30 days' notice of changes to prices or terms that affect a subscription" (`terms.md:46`). Read: a term "permitting ... one party ... to vary the terms of the contract" is s 25(d)'s example (VERIFIED) and "to vary the upfront price payable ... without the right of another party to terminate" is s 25(f) (VERIFIED); the cancel-at-any-time right answers (f), and notice answers part of (d), but the term does not say the customer may cancel without charge before the change takes effect. Severity: unclear. Text change: add that a subscriber who does not accept a change may cancel before it takes effect and is not charged for a later period. Why still the lawyer: whether "features" changes need notice at all under s 24(1)(b).

3.4 The paused-access sentence and the s 21 export carve-out (D-2415). Read: D-2415 makes the account export, with every worker table, available "whatever the subscription state" because the [WSR] s 21 record ("A record subject to this Part must be kept for 7 years from the date the record is made", VERIFIED) is the provider's and "must not sit behind a lapsed payment". `terms.md:30` says only that "access pauses". A reader whose payment failed and who needs the s 18 list for an auditor reads the terms as denying it; the product gives it. That is a representation narrower than the truth and a duty the product already meets. Severity: missing. Text change: add one sentence beside the pause sentence that the account export, including the worker records, stays available while access is paused. Why still the lawyer: none on this point; it is the PO's to add.

3.5 "Documents you have already bought stay available for download for at least 12 months after purchase" (`terms.md:46`). Read: a future-matter representation ([ACL] s 4, VERIFIED) with grounds in the export and the 30-day deletion window; sound while the retention rule holds. Severity: no finding. Text change: none.

3.6 "We may suspend an account that breaks these rules, and we will tell you why" (`terms.md:40`). Read: s 25(b) and (h) examples (one party "to terminate" and "unilaterally to determine whether the contract has been breached", VERIFIED); "we will tell you why" and the narrow rule list mitigate. Severity: unclear. Text change: add that a suspended customer keeps the export and may ask for the decision to be reviewed by email.

## 5. The privacy policy against the APPs and the small-business threshold

5.1 Is Readyline an APP entity? Read: on the stated facts, no, and the policy should say which. [PA] s 6C: an organisation is "an individual ... that is not a small business operator" (VERIFIED); s 6D(1): a small business is one whose "annual turnover for the previous financial year is $3,000,000 or less" (VERIFIED), with s 6D(2) for a new business (VERIFIED) and s 6DA counting "the proceeds of sales of goods and/or services" (VERIFIED). Of the s 6D(4) exceptions: (b) "provides a health service ... and holds any health information" does not fit Readyline (it collects none, CLAUDE.md 4.5); (e) is a Commonwealth contract, not present; (c) "discloses personal information about another individual to anyone else for a benefit, service or advantage" (VERIFIED) is the live one, because Readyline sends a sole trader's trading name, which is often the person's name, to Anthropic, Supabase, Vercel and Stripe in return for their services. s 6D(7) lifts (c) where the disclosure is "with the consent of the other individual" (VERIFIED), which the privacy policy's "Who helps us run the service" list and the sign-up acceptance may supply. The OAIC's small-business page, which usually says that paying a contractor to process data is not trading in it, was not reached (UNVERIFIED, 404 at the URL tried). Severity: for a practising lawyer, high, because every APP finding below turns on it. Text change: `privacy.md:62` "Some small businesses are not covered by the Privacy Act 1988. We follow the Australian Privacy Principles anyway" is true and should also say in one sentence whether Readyline has chosen to be treated as an organisation under s 6EA ("while a choice by the operator to be treated as an organisation is registered", VERIFIED), which is the one step that would make the promise bind under the Act. Why still the lawyer: s 6D(4)(c)'s construction and whether an s 6EA opt-in is wise given the notifiable-breach duty it brings.

5.2 If Readyline is not an APP entity, what binds it? Read: its own statements. Every sentence in `privacy.md` is "conduct ... in trade or commerce" under [ACL] s 18 (VERIFIED) and a representation that the services "have ... benefits" under s 29(1)(g) (VERIFIED); "We follow the Australian Privacy Principles" is then a promise enforced by the ACCC and the customer, not the OAIC, with the same content. The Commission's expectations bind the provider, not Readyline. Severity: for a practising lawyer, settled in direction. Text change: none beyond 5.1.

5.3 Overseas disclosure: the model provider and Stripe. Read: if an APP entity, [PA] APP 8.1 requires "such steps as are reasonable in the circumstances to ensure that the overseas recipient does not breach the Australian Privacy Principles" (VERIFIED) unless 8.2(a) (a "substantially similar" law with "mechanisms that the individual can access", VERIFIED) or 8.2(b) (express informed consent, VERIFIED) applies, and APP 1.4(g) wants "the countries in which such recipients are likely to be located if it is practicable" (VERIFIED). `privacy.md:42` names the United States for Anthropic; `:43` says Stripe "may process data outside Australia" with no country; `:41` says Vercel handles "some requests ... elsewhere briefly" with no country. Severity: missing (if an APP entity) or unclear (if not, as a half-stated representation). Text change: name the countries for Stripe and Vercel where Readyline can, or say that it cannot and why, in the "Who helps us run the service" list. Why still the lawyer: whether a United States recipient meets 8.2(a) (it generally does not, on the OAIC's published view, UNVERIFIED here) and so whether 8.1 steps or 8.2(b) consent is the route.

5.4 Retention. Read: `privacy.md:48` (30 days after deletion, tax records kept "for the period it requires", backups "on their normal schedule") matches APP 11.2's "destroy the information or ... ensure that the information is de-identified" once the entity "no longer needs the information" and no law requires retention (VERIFIED). Worker rows "while the account is open and never past the account's deletion" (D-2406) are consistent, and the policy rightly never says Readyline keeps the [WSR] s 18 list for the provider's seven years. Severity: no finding. Text change: none; when REQ-024 lands, the policy's worker lines say the seven-year record is the provider's own file (FR-6a's first line already does).

5.5 Access and correction. Read: APP 12.1 ("on request by the individual, give the individual access", VERIFIED), 12.4 ("within a reasonable period", "in the manner requested ... if it is reasonable and practicable", VERIFIED), 12.9 (a written refusal with reasons and complaint mechanisms, VERIFIED), APP 13.1 (correct on request or when satisfied the information is "inaccurate, out-of-date, incomplete, irrelevant or misleading", VERIFIED). `privacy.md:52-54` gives the owner export, delete and correct; it gives a worker nothing but the complaints line at `:66`. Severity: missing, for workers; the policy also lacks APP 1.4(e)'s "how the entity will deal with such a complaint" beyond "We reply within five business days". Text change: add a worker paragraph that names what a worker can see on their home and that anything else Readyline holds about them is given on request by email within a reasonable period, with a written reason if any part is refused.

5.6 The notifiable data breach sentence, `privacy.md:58` "If a breach happens that is likely to cause serious harm, we tell you and the regulator as the law requires". Read: [PA] s 26WE(1)(a) applies where "an APP entity holds personal information" (VERIFIED); a small business operator that has not opted in has no statutory duty, so "as the law requires" may require nothing. Severity: unclear. Text change: say "we tell you, and the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner where the law requires it", so the promise to the customer stands on its own and the regulator limb is conditional.

5.7 "If we find participant information, we may remove it and tell you" (`privacy.md:27`). Read: sound and consistent with APP 4 (unsolicited information: "destroy the information or ensure that the information is de-identified", VERIFIED) if an APP entity, and with the Code-covered provider's duty to "respect the privacy of people with disability" ([CoC] s 6(1)(b), VERIFIED). Severity: no finding. Text change: none.

5.8 The weekly encrypted backup held on GitHub under APP 8 (REQ-030, D-2871). Read: the backup workflow uploads an encrypted copy of the database to GitHub (United States) as a 90-day artifact; the key never enters GitHub, and GitHub cannot read the copy, so the PO's decision is that the upload is storage, not a disclosure to an overseas recipient, since APP 8 turns on the recipient being able to read the information (D-2871). The privacy page names GitHub, the country, the 90 days and the owner's key (D-2872) and the retention line says a copy may hold data for up to 90 days after deletion (D-2873). Severity: for a practising lawyer, to confirm. Question: does an encrypted copy the recipient cannot decrypt count as a disclosure under APP 8.1, or as the entity holding the information through a service provider under APP 11; and does the answer change if the repository is public (D-2876, SR-3)? Why still the lawyer: the OAIC's view on encrypted data held by a cloud provider was not reached here (UNVERIFIED).

## 6. The worker role, the one-time fill, the records exemption and the owner's notes (REQ-024)

6.1 Is the one-time fill (D-2408; FR-6b) a collection under APP 3 even though nothing is stored? Read: the safe reading is yes, as SR-8(i) assumes. [OAIC-B] B.26: an entity collects "only if the entity collects the personal information for inclusion in a record" and B.29: not "where that information is acquired but not included in a record" (both VERIFIED); the fill writes the names into a CSV, which is a record, before serving it and discarding it, so it is a collection for that moment. APP 3.2 (reasonably necessary for a function, VERIFIED) is met by the s 18 list; APP 3.5 ("only by lawful and fair means", VERIFIED) is met; APP 3.6 ("only from the individual unless ... (b) it is unreasonable or impracticable to do so", VERIFIED) is the limb the REQ never names, because the fill collects a worker's details from the owner, not the worker. Severity: missing, in the REQ's reasoning rather than the page. Text change: FR-6b's notice to the owner should say the owner supplies the details because collecting them from each worker for one file is impracticable, so the 3.6(b) reason is recorded where the collection happens. Why still the lawyer: whether 3.6(b) is satisfied for a current worker who could be asked directly.

6.2 Does the APP 5 notice reach the worker (D-2414; the printable notice)? Read: APP 5.1 allows notice "as soon as practicable after" collection (VERIFIED) and 5.1(b) "to otherwise ensure that the individual is aware" (VERIFIED); [OAIC-5] 5.6 names, as a reasonable step when collecting from another entity, "ensuring that the other entity has notified or made the individual aware ... on its behalf (such as through an enforceable contractual arrangement)" (VERIFIED). The owner's required tick is the mechanism; it is weaker than a contract unless the terms of use make the tick a term. The printable notice covers 5.2(a), (b), (d), (f), and must also carry (i) and (j), whether the details go overseas: they do not (the fill "runs in Sydney", SR-1), and the notice should say so. Severity: unclear. Text change: add to `terms.md` one sentence that an owner who fills worker details agrees to tell those workers as the export screen asks, and add to the printable notice that the details do not leave Australia.

6.3 The employee-records exemption, [PA] s 7B(3), for contractors and volunteers (SR-1(a), (e)). Read: the exemption is for "an organisation that is or was an employer of an individual" where the act "is directly related to: (a) a current or former employment relationship ...; and (b) an employee record held by the organisation" (VERIFIED), and an employee record is "in relation to an employee" (s 6, VERIFIED). Two consequences follow from the words: Readyline is nobody's employer, so s 7B(3) never exempts Readyline for anything it holds; and the provider's own s 18 list is exempt only for employees, so a contractor's or volunteer's entry is personal information the provider handles under the APPs if the provider is an APP entity. Whether the provider is one does not depend on turnover alone: s 6FB(3)(b) makes an activity "in the course of providing ... care for a person with a disability" a health service (VERIFIED) and s 6D(4)(b) removes the small-business status of one who "provides a health service to another individual and holds any health information except in an employee record" (VERIFIED), which a personal-care or SIL provider holding participant health information does. Severity: missing, in the generated documents (REQ-005): the generated privacy policy and the worker screening procedure should carry one paragraph on worker information for people who are not employees. Text change: as SR-1(e) proposes, one paragraph, and the generated privacy policy should say that the provider treats itself as covered by the Privacy Act where it holds participant health information. Why still the lawyer: whether "holds any health information" is met by every provider the questionnaire admits (a transport-only or household-tasks provider may hold none).

6.4 The APP 12 access path for a worker (SR-1(b), (f)) and the owner's notes. Read: Readyline "holds" what is in its database ("possession or control of a record", s 6, VERIFIED; [OAIC-B] B.82 to B.84, VERIFIED), so the owner's `note` fields, the supervision notes and the `register_events.note` lines about a worker are personal information Readyline holds about the worker, whoever typed them. APP 12.1 gives the worker access on request; [OAIC-12] 12.3 lets an "informal administrative arrangement" (the worker home through the two views, D-2429) satisfy it "provided the minimum access requirements ... have been met" (VERIFIED), and 12.4 and 12.9 govern the rest. The exceptions that could cover an owner's note are 12.3(b) ("unreasonable impact on the privacy of other individuals", VERIFIED) and 12.3(d) (anticipated legal proceedings, VERIFIED); 12.3(j) ("evaluative information ... commercially sensitive decision-making process", VERIFIED) is a poor fit for a supervision note. Who answers: if Readyline is an APP entity it answers for what it holds; if it is not, its promise to "follow the APPs anyway" answers under the ACL; the provider answers for its own copy either way. Severity: for a practising lawyer, medium. Text change: the candidate worker line SR-1 records ("your home shows what we hold about you except your manager's notes; ask us for those") is consistent with 12.1 and 12.3 and can go in once the lawyer confirms the division; no promise of an outcome in it.
