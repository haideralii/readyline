## 1. Testability

103. FR-2: "every change to it also appends one tick and one `register_events` row", and FR-8: "an item the worker ticks shows 'waiting for your manager to confirm' until the owner confirms ... The trail holds both dates and both actors". `onboarding_ticks` is `unique (checklist_id, item_id)` (`20261014100000_audit_day_registers.sql:242`), and the Dev's answer 1 relies on it ("each existing item has at most one tick"). The worker's tick and the owner's confirmation on the same item are two ticks; the second fails with 23505, so FR-8's "both dates and both actors" and AC-3's "the trail holds both" cannot pass, and every later change to an item (a new expiry typed) fails the same way. Fix: the items migration drops that unique key and adds `unique (checklist_id, item_id, ticked_by_ref, ticked_on)` (or the tick table gains a `kind` of done/confirmed with the key on it), Data says so, and the Dev's answer 1 reads the latest tick per actor.
104. AC-6: "when it runs `update` on its own `onboarding_items` row for any column, then the write is refused with 42501". Under row-level security with no update policy for the worker role and the `update` privilege granted to `authenticated` (the owner needs it), Postgres applies the policies as a filter: zero rows match, zero rows change, no error. 42501 appears only when a `with check` fails or when the privilege itself is missing (the D-906 pattern on the append-only tables, where `update` is revoked from `authenticated`). Fix: AC-6 asserts "zero rows updated and the row unchanged on re-read" for the worker's direct update (and 42501 only for the tick insert with another worker's `ticked_by_ref`, which a `with check` does refuse), or Data revokes `update` on `onboarding_items` from `authenticated` and routes the owner's writes through the same `security definer` pair, which keeps 42501 for both roles.
105. Data: "a status view `staff_matrix_status` with the derived state and the next item, computed against a `today` the caller passes". A view takes no argument; the existing `staff_matrix_status` reads `current_date` (`20261014100000_audit_day_registers.sql:315-333`) and `audit-day-forms.ts:866` names it as the register's view. Nothing in the REQ says where the derivation runs. Fix: the derived state is a pure TypeScript function over the worker's rows (`today` in, the D-2242 rule; the unit tests the REQ lists already assume this) and the view keeps its two counts, or Data names a SQL function `staff_matrix_status_at(p_account uuid, p_today date)` with its grants; AC-2 and AC-14 then say which they call.
106. AC-11: "Given the worker's first sign-in, when the home renders, then the collection notice screen shows first and the home shows after its one button". Nothing records that the notice was seen; "first" across two devices or a cleared browser needs a stored fact, and a worker has no update path to any table (D-2417). Fix: Data adds `memberships.notice_seen_at timestamptz` set by one more `security definer` function the button calls (or a column on the worker's own `staff_matrix` row written by `worker_update_item`'s sibling), and AC-11 asserts the column and that the second render skips the screen.
107. AC-13: "Given W-2 in a risk assessed role ... W-3 ... W-5 an admin": W-1 to W-10 are the item ids everywhere else in the REQ, and here they are worker codes. And the `role-changed` event's `at` is the server's time of the edit; an owner who records a reclassification a week late gives the builder the wrong day, and the cleared `risk_assessed_from` leaves no start date behind.
