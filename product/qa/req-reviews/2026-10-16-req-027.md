## 8. Domain: the NDIS expert's read (D-2222)

44. FR-2 forces "key personnel: the people who run the business" beside the workforce step's screening question (`workforce-step.tsx`, two uses). "Key personnel" is a defined term in the NDIS Act 2013 (s 11A) covering the governing body, the CEO and anyone who makes or takes part in decisions affecting the whole or a substantial part of the business; "the people who run the business" under-includes a non-owner manager, and a wrong meaning beside a question changes an answer that drives the screening result (REQ-024 W-1). UNVERIFIED by fetch (legislation.gov.au, NDIS Act 2013 s 11A; the review could not reach it). Fix: VERIFY against s 11A before the meaning sits on a questionnaire step, or exempt the questionnaire steps from FR-3 until it is.
45. Sound: "an answer is correct when it names the audit type and says documents will be drafted" matches what the page can truthfully say (the pathway from `audit-types.json`, the pack as drafts for review). Suggestion: add "and does not say the documents make them registered or audit-ready" as the one wrong answer that fails the page rather than the tester, because that is the misreading the Commission's audit process punishes (a borrowed policy nobody follows, REQ-025's research).
46. Sound: the audit-kind and mid-term meanings the result page spreads are consistent with the repo's own primer (`ndis-registration-primer.md`: verification for lower-risk groups, mid-term for certification about 18 months in). Suggestion: the "What these words mean" disclosure on a guide lists the term in the Commission's spelling ("registration group or class", as `which-ndis-audit.md:16` does), since the 2026 rules use "class" for the mandatory-registration groups (CLAUDE.md section 2) and a reader who searched for "class 0138" must find the word.
47. Imprecise: "Practice Standards: the rules every registered provider is audited against" on pages for unregistered readers (the home page, the guides). A provider registering by choice is audited against the modules that apply, not "the rules"; the meaning is fine on the result page where the modules are listed under it and thin on a guide. Suggestion: the disclosure on a guide appears under the guide's "short answer" so the modules sentence is already on screen.
