## The two reads, framing

Lawyer's read (questions for the practising lawyer, not advice). The disclosure opens "Our plain words, not the Commission's". That is a useful qualification, but ACL s 18 cannot be contracted or disclaimed out of, and the meanings are read by a person deciding whether to pay for a product that says it helps them pass an audit. Each meaning is therefore a representation about how the regime works; where it describes what will happen at the reader's own audit (a visit or no visit, a check "halfway through your three years") it is a representation about a future matter, and ACL s 4 asks whether there are reasonable grounds for it. The safe pattern is: say what the law says, attribute it ("the Rules say"), and never state as a fact something the Commissioner can vary. Privacy Act: the glossary holds no personal information; nothing to raise. NDIS Act: the only names used are statutory or the Commission's own ("NDIS Worker Screening Check", "Practice Standards"); the lawyer should confirm this nominative use of "NDIS" is fine on a commercial site (the primer notes the NDIA owns the NDIS trade mark).

NDIS expert's read: each meaning checked against the Act, the Rules and the Guidelines quoted above. Where the official text uses a different word from the glossary, the official word wins, and the plain-words proposal keeps the official word in brackets when the reader will meet it on the Commission's portal.

House rule for the proposals: sentences under 20 words on average, everyday words, one idea each, true for a reader not yet registered and for one who is, no promised outcome.

## 1. verification audit

Current: "an auditor checks your documents at a desk; nobody visits"

Lawyer's read: loophole. "Nobody visits" is an absolute statement about a future matter. The Rules define verification as a desk audit but do not forbid a visit, and a provider with one certification class is audited by certification for the lot (Rules s 23 and s 20(3)). Fix: replace "nobody visits" with the Rules' own word, "desk audit", and say the Commissioner or the auditor can ask for more.

NDIS expert's read: imprecise. Rules s 4: "verification means an assessment by an approved quality auditor of an applicant, or of a registered NDIS provider, against an applicable standard by conducting a desk audit of the applicant or provider, including reviewing the applicant's or provider's relevant documentation, in relation to the standard." The auditor is an approved quality auditor (Act s 9: "approved quality auditor means a person or body approved by the Commissioner under section 73U"). The standards checked are those in Schedule 8, Module 6: Verification (risk management, incident management, complaints management, human resource management), not the core module. "Nobody visits" is a gloss the Rules do not make.

Proposed: "an approved auditor checks your documents without coming to see you. The Rules call it a desk audit. It applies to lower-risk supports."

## 2. certification audit

Current: "an auditor reads your documents and visits you"

Lawyer's read: sound in direction, but it understates what the reader signs up for, and an understated audit is a representation too (a reader could decide on this product believing interviews with participants are not part of the audit). Fix: add the interviews, which the Rules make a required part.

NDIS expert's read: imprecise. Rules s 5(1): "Certification is an assessment by an approved quality auditor ... by conducting: (a) a desk audit ...; and (b) an inspection of the sites, facilities, equipment and services used, or proposed to be used, in the delivery of supports or services ...; and (c) interviews with relevant persons, including key personnel of the applicant or provider and persons receiving, or to receive, supports or services from the applicant or provider". s 5(1A): the assessment "must be proportionate to" the provider's size, scale and the scope and complexity of the supports; s 5(2): sampling is allowed. The glossary omits the interviews and the proportionality.

Proposed: "an approved auditor reads your documents, visits where you work and talks to people. That includes you, your workers and the people you support. It applies to higher-risk supports."

## 3. Practice Standards

Current: "the rules every registered provider is audited against"

Lawyer's read: loophole. The sentence is false for a verification provider, who is audited against the verification module only, and for an SDA-only provider, who is not audited against the core module. A reader on the pricing page who is told these are the rules "every" provider is audited against may buy a core-module pack they do not need. Fix: say the audit covers the standards that apply to the reader's supports.

NDIS expert's read: wrong, as the brief anticipated. Act s 73T(1): "The National Disability Insurance Scheme rules may make provision for or in relation to standards concerning the quality of supports or services to be provided by registered NDIS providers"; s 73T(2): "Rules made for the purposes of subsection (1) are to be known as the NDIS Practice Standards." Rules s 4: "applicable standards means the NDIS Practice Standards that apply to a specified class of supports under Part 6". Rules s 20(1): an applicant must "be assessed by an approved quality auditor, using the method specified in column 3 of that item, as meeting each standard that: (i) is specified in a Schedule mentioned in column 2 of that item; and (ii) applies to the provider". Rules s 20(2): to remain registered a provider "must comply with the standards specified in column 2". So the standards are a set of which only the applicable ones reach a given provider, they bind the provider between audits (non-compliance is a breach of a condition of registration, s 73T note, s 73F(2)(c)), and they bind applicants as well as providers (Schedule 1 cl 1(1)).

Proposed: "the quality rules in the law for registered providers. Which rules apply to you depends on the supports you provide or apply for. Your audit checks those."

## 4. module

Current: "a group of those rules that applies to the supports you deliver"

Lawyer's read: sound. One small loophole: "you deliver" reads as present tense, so a reader who is not yet registered may think modules attach only once they are delivering. Fix: "deliver or apply to deliver".

NDIS expert's read: right in substance, imprecise at the edge. The Rules' Schedules are titled "Schedule 1—Core module", "Schedule 2—Module 1: High intensity daily personal activities", through "Schedule 7A—Module 5A: Assistance with supported independent living" and "Schedule 8—Module 6: Verification" (compilation 6). Modules attach to a class of supports through the s 20(3) table, with one exception the glossary does not need to carry: Schedule 4 (Module 2A, implementing behaviour support plans) attaches to the use of a regulated restrictive practice, not to a class (s 20(1)(b)). The core module applies to every certification class except SDA-only. "Those rules" relies on the reader having read item 3 first; the disclosure lists them in order, so that holds.

Proposed: "a group of those rules. Which modules apply to you depends on the supports you deliver or apply for."

## 5. quality indicator

Current: "one thing an auditor looks for under a rule"

Lawyer's read: sound. No promise, no absolute, and it says what it is without saying what will happen.

NDIS expert's read: right. Rules s 24: "The quality indicators set out in the National Disability Insurance Scheme (Quality Indicators for NDIS Practice Standards) Guidelines 2018 (as existing from time to time) must be taken into account in assessing the following: (a) whether an applicant for registration as a registered NDIS provider meets the NDIS Practice Standards; (b) whether a registered NDIS provider is complying with those standards." Guidelines s 5: "This Division sets out the quality indicators for the NDIS Practice Standards about rights of participants and responsibilities of providers"; the Guidelines' tables map each set of indicators to one clause of the Rules' Schedules. An indicator is one of several signs under a standard that the auditor must take into account. "Under a rule" matches that mapping. Covers applicants as well as providers (s 24(a)).

Proposed (optional, same meaning with the "must take into account" nuance): "one of the signs an auditor must look at to decide whether you meet a rule". Can ship as it stands.

## 6. registration group

Current (term "registration group"): "the Commission's name for a type of support"

Lawyer's read: loophole in the term, not the sentence. The law's word is "class of supports"; the certificate and the Rules' table use it. A reader who looks for "registration group" on their certificate will not find it. The brief's rename to "registration group or class" closes this. Fix: rename the term and say both words are in use.

NDIS expert's read: imprecise. Act s 73E(5)(b): the certificate must specify "the classes of supports or services the person is registered to provide"; Act s 73E(2)(b): a person may be registered for "providing specified classes of supports under participants' plans". Rules s 20(3) table, column 1: "To be registered, or remain registered, to provide this class of supports…", with items such as "accommodation/tenancy assistance" and "assistance with supported independent living" (s 7(5)). "Registration group" with its four-digit code is the Commission's and the NDIA's label; the Commission's apply page says "the registration groups or classes of support you're applying for" (read 16 Oct 2026). Neither "registration group" nor a code appears in the Act or the Rules.

Proposed (term renamed "registration group or class"): "a type of support you are registered, or apply, to provide. The law says 'class of supports'. The Commission's lists say 'registration group', with a four-digit code."

## 7. mid-term audit

Current: "a check about halfway through your three years"

Lawyer's read: loophole, the largest in the set. Three claims, each a future-matter representation: that the reader has a three-year period (the Commissioner may set a shorter or longer one, Act s 73L(3)(b); REQ-023 R-1), that the audit is "halfway" (the Rules say it must commence by 18 months, so a provider with a two-year period who waits until "halfway" at 12 months is fine, but one who reads "halfway" as "around month 18" has already run out of time, and one who thinks it is a soft "check" may not book an approved auditor), and that it applies to "your" registration (it does not apply to verification providers or the s 13B(7) exempt providers, and a reader not yet registered has no period at all). Fix: attribute the 18 months to the Rules, say who it applies to, and drop "three years".

NDIS expert's read: wrong. Rules s 13B(1): "This section applies in relation to a registered NDIS provider that is registered to provide a class of supports for which, under the table in subsection 20(3), the assessment method for the applicable standards is certification." s 13B(3): "The audit must be carried out by an approved quality auditor using certification." s 13B(4): "The audit must commence no later than: (a) 18 months after the beginning of the period for which the provider's registration is in force; or (b) such longer period after the beginning of that period as the Commissioner allows." s 13B(5): it assesses "the standards in Part 3 of Schedule 1" (provider governance and operational management), any standard under a corrective action plan, and any standard the Commissioner names in a notice. s 13B(7): it does not apply to an individual or partnership whose only certification class is early intervention supports for early childhood, to an SDA-only provider, or to a transitioned provider. The period is "the period for which the registration is in force" on the certificate (Act s 73E(5)(e)); three years is usual, not a fact of law (REQ-023 R-1). "Check" undersells a certification audit by an approved quality auditor; "halfway" and "three years" are not in the Rules.

Proposed: "a second audit that most certification providers must have. The Rules say it must start within 18 months of your registration period starting, unless the Commissioner allows longer. It covers your governance and management rules."

## 8. restrictive practice

Current: "a practice that restricts a person's rights or movement; the law controls it" (flagged VERIFY against Act s 9)

Lawyer's read: sound in substance; one loophole in "a person", which widens the term beyond the Act (the Act is about a person with disability) and so could make a provider think a practice applied to a worker or a visitor is a reportable matter, and one in "the law controls it", which is true but says nothing a reader can act on. Fix: "a person with disability", and "the law limits when and how it may be used".

NDIS expert's read: right in substance, imprecise in two words. Act s 9, compilation 28: "restrictive practice means any practice or intervention that has the effect of restricting the rights or freedom of movement of a person with disability." The glossary drops "with disability", drops "intervention" and narrows "freedom of movement" to "movement". The VERIFY tag can be cleared once the words below are adopted. The Rules s 4 define the five "regulated restrictive practice" kinds (seclusion, chemical, mechanical, physical and environmental restraint), and s 20(1)(b) attaches Schedule 4 (Module 2A) to any provider using them; the glossary need not list the five, but the questionnaire's restrictive-practice input should keep pointing at "regulated restrictive practice" as the trigger for the module.

Proposed: "anything done that restricts the rights or freedom of movement of a person with disability. The law limits when and how it may be used."

## 9. key personnel

Current: "the people who run the business"

Lawyer's read: loophole. Key personnel suitability is a registration criterion (Act s 73E(1)(e)), the apply page asks for each one's name, date of birth and contact details and states "your application is an accurate representation of your organisation and key personnel", and a joining or leaving director or manager is a s 13A(1)(d) notification (REQ-023 R-4). A reader who takes "the people who run the business" as "the owner and the manager" may leave out a non-executive director, a silent partner with a veto, or a relative who in practice directs the work, and so lodge an incomplete application. Fix: add the s 11A(1)(b) limb in plain words, "anyone else with a real say in how it is run".

NDIS expert's read: imprecise, misses s 11A(1)(b). Act s 11A(1): "Each of the following is one of the key personnel of a person or entity: (a) a member of the group of persons who is responsible for the executive decisions of the person or entity; (b) any other person who has authority or responsibility for (or significant influence over) planning, directing or controlling the activities of the person or entity." s 11A(2): paragraph (a) includes "a director of the body corporate" under the Corporations Act 2001 and, in any other case, "a member of the person's or entity's governing body". Key personnel roles are also risk assessed roles for worker screening (Worker Screening Rules s 4, "risk assessed role means: (a) a key personnel role of a person or an entity"), which the glossary's worker-screening item should not contradict (see item 11).

Proposed: "the people who make the big decisions in the business, such as directors or partners. Anyone else with a real say in how it is run counts too."

## 10. self-assessment

Current: "the form where you say how you meet each rule"

Lawyer's read: sound in direction, two loopholes. "Each rule" repeats item 3's error (it is each applicable standard), and the sentence omits the evidence the Commission asks for, so a reader may think a written statement is enough. Fix: "each rule that applies to you, with evidence". Also note for the lawyer: the self-assessment is Commission practice, not a provision of the Rules, so the meaning should not say "the law requires".

NDIS expert's read: imprecise. The term is not in the Act or the Rules; it is a step in the Commission's application. Apply page (read 16 Oct 2026): the application includes "a self-assessment against the applicable NDIS Practice Standards, including evidence to support comments". Renew page (read 16 Oct 2026): a renewal includes "a self-assessment against the applicable NDIS practice standards and answering questions about your suitability". The portal then produces the "Initial scope of audit document" the provider gives to auditors for quotes (apply page; primer step 3). So: part of the online application, against the applicable standards, with evidence, used at first registration and at renewal.

Proposed: "the part of the Commission's online application where you say, with evidence, how you meet each rule that applies to you. Renewals have one too."

## 11. NDIS Worker Screening Check

Current: "the national police-and-history check a worker in a risk-assessed role must hold"

Lawyer's read: loophole on three fronts. "Must hold" is an absolute that the Worker Screening Rules qualify (a worker may work in a risk assessed role while an application is in progress, supervised, under a risk management plan, where the state's law allows: s 14(1)(a)); a provider who reads "must hold" could unlawfully stand a worker down, or, reading the exceptions elsewhere, decide the glossary is unreliable. "National" could be read as a Commonwealth check; it is run by each state and territory under its own law and recognised across Australia. "A worker" leaves out that the sole-trader provider and the key personnel need the clearance too. Fix: say "clearance", say who runs it, say "with few exceptions".

NDIS expert's read: imprecise. Act s 9: "NDIS worker screening check means an assessment, under an NDIS worker screening law, of whether a person who works, or seeks to work, with people with disability poses a risk to such people"; "NDIS worker screening law means a law of a State or Territory determined in an instrument under subsection 10B(1)". Worker Screening Rules s 4: "clearance means a decision made under the NDIS worker screening law of a participating jurisdiction: (a) in response to an application for an NDIS worker screening check; (b) having the effect that the person who made the application is cleared to work with people with disability in a risk assessed role"; "risk assessed role means: (a) a key personnel role of a person or an entity; (b) a role for which the normal duties include the direct delivery of specified supports or specified services to a person with disability; or (c) a role for which the normal duties are likely to require more than incidental contact with a person with disability". s 13: a registered NDIS provider "(a) must only allow a worker to engage in a risk assessed role, if the worker has a clearance; ... (c) if the registered NDIS provider is an individual—must only engage in a risk assessed role if the registered NDIS provider has a clearance", "Subject to the exceptions contained in this Division". Commission factsheet (read 16 Oct 2026): "The NDIS Worker Screening Check (Worker Screening Check) is a check conducted by state and territory Worker Screening Units"; "The check determines whether a person is cleared or excluded from working in certain roles"; "NDIS Worker Screening clearances expire every five (5) years"; the check is "based on nationally consistent criteria" and lets a worker work "in any state or territory across Australia". So the thing a worker holds is a clearance, the check is the assessment, "police-and-history" is a gloss (the Act says "whether a person ... poses a risk"), and the duty is the registered provider's (s 13), with exceptions (s 14).

Proposed: "a check by your state or territory of whether a person is a risk to people with disability. Workers in risk-assessed roles, including key personnel and sole traders, need the clearance it gives, with few exceptions. Clearances last up to five years."

## 12. reportable incident

Current: "a serious incident the Commission must be told about"

Lawyer's read: loophole. "Serious" is the reader's judgement, and the Act's list is not a seriousness test: an unauthorised restrictive practice that caused no harm is a reportable incident, and a provider who filters by "serious" will miss it (the Incident Rules give it a five-business-day route, primer "Rules the registers must respect"). The passive "must be told" hides that the duty is the registered provider's, which matters on a public page read by unregistered providers who have no reportable-incident duty under s 73Z but do have the Code of Conduct. Fix: say "an incident on the law's list", name the list, and say who reports.

NDIS expert's read: imprecise. Act s 9: "reportable incident has the meaning given by subsections 73Z(4) and (5)". s 73Z(4): "Reportable incident means: (a) the death of a person with disability; or (b) serious injury to a person with disability; or (c) abuse or neglect of a person with disability; or (d) unlawful sexual or physical contact with, or assault of, a person with disability; or (e) sexual misconduct committed against, or in the presence of, a person with disability, including grooming of the person for sexual activity; or (f) the use of a restrictive practice in relation to a person with disability, other than where the use is in accordance with an authorisation (however described) of a State or Territory in relation to the person." s 73Z(5) lets the rules add or remove items. s 73Z(1) ties the notifying and managing duty to incidents "in connection with the provision of supports or services by registered NDIS providers". The Act's own word is "notify" (the Commission's practice word is "report"); either is fine in plain words.

Proposed: "an incident of a kind the law lists, which a registered provider must report to the Commission. The list covers death, serious injury, abuse or neglect, sexual or physical assault or misconduct, and a restrictive practice used without authorisation."

## 13. adopt

Current: "sign and date a document so it is yours"

Lawyer's read: sound. This is the product's own word with no official meaning, and the disclosure's opening line says so. One question for the lawyer rather than a loophole: on the pricing and result pages, does "so it is yours" risk implying that adopting a generated document meets the standard it is written for? The primer's failure list ("policies that do not match what the provider actually does", "no evidence of staff reading policies") is the reason to add one plain sentence that it does not. If the lawyer wants it closed, the fix is the second sentence below.

NDIS expert's read: right as a house word; nothing in the Act, the Rules or the Guidelines defines "adopt". The thing an auditor tests is the standard and its quality indicators (Rules s 24), for which a signed document is evidence, and practice is the other half.

Proposed: "sign and date a document so it is yours. An auditor also looks for proof you follow it." Can ship as it stands; the second sentence is optional and recommended.

## NDIS expert's named step before `status: reviewed` (REQ-027 D-2709, Domain rules; PO, 16 Oct 2026)

The step the REQ names is done against the texts the repository and the review folder hold, not Commission pages: the Worker Screening Rules at compilation No. 4 (31 July 2021, amendments to F2021L01050), the Incident Rules as made (F2018L00633, registered 17 May 2018; the register showed no later compilation on 16 October 2026), the Practice Standards Rules at compilation No. 6 (1 July 2026, amendments to F2026L00802) and the Act's s 9. Each line below is VERIFIED from the quoted text unless it says otherwise.

1. The Incident Rules' addition (finding 108(a), decision 13 of the third pass): VERIFIED. s 16(3): "Despite paragraph 73Z(4)(f) of the Act, the use of a restrictive practice in relation to a person with disability where the use is in accordance with an authorisation (however described) of a State or Territory is a reportable incident if the use is not in accordance with a behaviour support plan for the person with disability." The Rules also remove a case, s 16(4): a use not in accordance with an authorisation "is not a reportable incident if: (a) the use is in accordance with a behaviour support plan ...; and (b) the State or Territory ... does not have authorisation process", and s 16(2) removes negligible unlawful physical contact. So "an incident the law and the Rules list" is true as typed, and item 12's `more` should carry s 16(3) rather than the Act's limb alone; the wording is D-2729 (REQ-027).

2. "risk assessed role" (Rules s 4): VERIFIED, "(a) a key personnel role of a person or an entity; (b) a role for which the normal duties include the direct delivery of specified supports or specified services to a person with disability; or (c) a role for which the normal duties are likely to require more than incidental contact with a person with disability". s 13(c): VERIFIED, "if the registered NDIS provider is an individual—must only engage in a risk assessed role if the registered NDIS provider has a clearance", under the opening words "Subject to the exceptions contained in this Division". s 14: VERIFIED as quoted in line 3. D-2716's sole-trader line, "A sole trader needs the clearance in their own name.", and its source line, "Worker Screening Rules s 13(c), with few exceptions.", state the rule as the text has it (findings 124, 133, 138).

3. The "few exceptions" the source line means (finding 186): s 14(1)(a), all of "(i) the person is in the process of obtaining a clearance; (ii) the person is appropriately supervised by a person with a clearance; (iii) the registered NDIS provider is implementing a risk management plan developed and maintained in accordance with Division 3; (iv) the law of the participating jurisdiction ... allows the person to engage in a risk assessed role while that person is in the process of obtaining a clearance", which is item 11's lawyer's read at line 142 of this file; for the individual provider the mirror is s 14(2)(a). The other limbs are s 14(1)(b) and s 14(2)(b) (the Part 4 transitional arrangements, which REQ-024 D-2426 dates) and s 14(1)(c) (a supervised secondary-school work-experience student). s 14A withdraws the s 14(1)(a) and (2)(a) exception in a "no card, no start" jurisdiction. One named referent, s 14(1)(a), stands for the source line; the lawyer role's read (`product/legal/questions-for-the-lawyer.md` 8.1, 8.3) adds that Victoria and Queensland do not allow the pending-application work for a first application, which REQ-024's state lines carry, not the glossary.

4. "clearance" against "check" (finding 187): VERIFIED. Rules s 4, "clearance means a decision made under the NDIS worker screening law of a participating jurisdiction: (a) in response to an application for an NDIS worker screening check; (b) having the effect that the person who made the application is cleared to work with people with disability in a risk assessed role". Act s 9, "NDIS worker screening check means an assessment, under an NDIS worker screening law, of whether a person who works, or seeks to work, with people with disability poses a risk to such people". The glossary's `term` is the check (the assessment) and "the clearance it gives" in item 10's `more` is the decision; the three clearance forms in the forms table are forms of the same entry, which is right because a reader who meets either word needs the one meaning.

5. Item 10's `more` read with s 13(a) to (c) (finding 176): "Workers in risk-assessed roles need the clearance it gives" is s 13(a) ("must only allow a worker to engage in a risk assessed role, if the worker has a clearance"); "and so do key personnel" is s 4(a)'s first limb of "risk assessed role" with s 13(a) and (b) (personnel through a personnel provider, s 13(b)(iii) "reasonable steps to satisfy itself that the individual has a clearance"); "and sole-trader providers" is s 13(c); "with few exceptions" is s 14. "Clearances last up to five years": not in the Rules; it is the Commission factsheet line quoted in item 11 above ("expire every five (5) years") and the state Acts set the term; it stays as a Commission statement, read 16 October 2026.
