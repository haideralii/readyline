# Role review 3 of 7: field specialist (a support worker or team leader who has to act on these documents on shift)

- **Environment, SHA, devices, account:** as in `qa.md`; the pack is the fresh fixture render of the sole-trader set (Sunrise Supports, household tasks, NSW, verification), read as the worker Sunrise might take on next month would read it on a phone; the registers were tried on the Android capture (360 px) with one incident entered.
- **Read first:** `qa.md` and `product-manager.md`. Where I repeat them I say so; where I disagree: QA calls the missing "words we use" (their 10) major until re-recorded; for me it is "I would get it wrong" on the night, which is worse than major.
- **Severity scale:** "I could not do my job" / "I would get it wrong" / "annoying".

## The 2 am walks

**Moment 1: the participant has a seizure that is on their medical emergency list.** I open the Incident management policy. Section 8 Incident procedure, step 1: "Make sure everyone is safe. Stop the household task if there is any risk. Call 000 first if anyone is in danger or needs urgent medical help." Step 2: "Look after the participant. Stay with them... Tell their family, guardian or nominee if the participant wants us to, or if they need to know." That gets me to 000 and staying. What it does not give me is the participant's own emergency list: the procedure never says "check the participant's medical emergency list in their support plan" (the batch 40 brief asks for that step; the fixture predates it). Then "Write the incident in the incident register on the same day." Clear. Verdict: I get there, but I would not know there is a list to check. **I would get it wrong** (I would treat every seizure as new).

**Moment 2: a family member shouts at me and says they will complain.** Complaints policy, section 7, step 1: "Receive the complaint. Anyone can complain: a participant, a family member... We do not need a form." Step 2: "Acknowledge it. We thank the person, confirm we have understood..." Step 4 asks what they want. Good: I know the first move is to listen and thank them. What I cannot find is a sentence to say. The brief asked for a "Words we use" callout; this recording has none. The Commission's number is now right there (the fixed "How to contact the Commission" block after the procedure), and the tailored text two paragraphs earlier also gives numbers, including an interpreter number nobody sourced. Verdict: I get to "listen, thank, record, tell the owner", with no script. **Annoying** on the script; **I would get it wrong** on which number to hand over (see finding 3).

**Moment 3: I notice a locked door that keeps a participant in.** Safeguarding policy, "Our position on restrictive practices" (fixed text): "A locked door, a withheld phone or snack, or a held arm can be a restrictive practice even when nobody meant it to be. We treat it as one." Then the four steps: keep the person safe, tell the owner the same day and record it in the incident register before the end of the shift, the owner reports within 5 business days or 24 hours if harmed, the owner reviews within 5 business days. That is the clearest page in the pack. Verdict: I know what to do and by when. **Passes.**

## Findings

1. **Where:** Incident management policy, section 7 "Who tells whom, and by when" (fixed) versus section 8 "Incident procedure" (drafted). **Seen:** the table's last-but-one row: "The owner tells the participant and the people they choose, the same day, with the open-disclosure words in this procedure." The procedure has no open-disclosure words anywhere. **Expected:** the sentence I say when something went wrong on my watch ("I am sorry this happened. Here is what I know, here is what I am doing, here is what happens next."). **Severity:** I would get it wrong (I would say "the owner will be in touch" and leave). **Fix:** re-record the procedure with the "Words we use" callout the brief asks for; until then the table row should not promise words that are not there (QA 10 says the same).
2. **Where:** Incident management policy, section 8, the step table. **Seen:** "Find the cause and decide actions: Owner: As soon as possible after the incident." **Expected:** a number ("within 2 business days"), like every other row. **Severity:** annoying for me, I would get it wrong for the owner (nothing is ever "as soon as possible" when you are the only worker). **Fix:** on the recording list (vague-time lint); the house style already forbids it.
3. **Where:** Complaints policy, section 7, last paragraph of the drafted text and the fixed block that follows it. **Seen:** drafted: "They can phone the Commission on 1800 035 544 (free call from landlines; TTY 133 677; interpreter service on 131 450) or go to... ndiscommission.gov.au". Fixed block: phone, hours, TTY, National Relay Service, email, website. Two lists, one with an interpreter number the other does not have, no hours in the first and hours in the second. **Expected:** one list, and I hand the participant the one with the hours. **Severity:** I would get it wrong (I would read out the first one and the office is shut). **Fix:** re-record the procedure under the new brief (it now says refer to the fixed section by heading); the contact lint will stop the numbers coming back.
4. **Where:** Incident management policy, section 9 "Incident register" (fixed template). **Seen:** columns "Reference | Date received | Summary and actions | Outcome and improvement" with the example row "[C-001] [Date]". The app's incident form asks me for the category, how serious, whether it is reportable, the date I told the Commission and the status; the pack's paper template asks for none of those, and the example code is a complaint code. **Expected:** the paper register and the app's register ask the same things, and the example reads INC-001. **Severity:** I would get it wrong (on paper I would never write "reportable: yes", which is the one thing the Commission wants to see). **Fix:** give the incident register template the app's columns (date happened, date reported, what happened, how serious, reportable yes/no, told the Commission on, status) and an INC- example; same for the complaints template (date received, how it arrived, acknowledged on, outcome, closed on).
5. **Where:** Safeguarding policy, section 7 "Responding to an allegation", steps 3 and 4. **Seen:** "Write down what was said... Do not put the record anywhere other than the incident register." then "Record the allegation in the incident register and open an incident file, following our incident management procedure." **Expected:** one instruction: write it in the incident register, and if the owner opens a file for it, the owner does. **Severity:** I would get it wrong (step 3 says only the register, step 4 says a file too). **Fix:** drop "and open an incident file" or say "the owner opens the file".
6. **Where:** Incident management policy, section 7 table versus section 6 table. **Seen:** the escalation table's roles are "The worker on shift", "The key person on duty", "The owner" and it says "The role names match the responsibilities table"; the responsibilities table's only role is "Owner and sole worker". **Expected:** for a sole trader the table should collapse to one line or say "when you are the only worker, you are all three". **Severity:** I would get it wrong on the day Sunrise takes me on: who is "the key person on duty" when the owner is off? **Fix:** the fixed table takes the role names from the document's responsibilities table, or carries the one-worker sentence.
7. **Where:** Worker screening and recruitment procedure, section 6 versus section 4. **Seen:** "Sunrise Supports checks this register each month" (section 6) and "Reviews the register for expired, expiring or missing clearances: Every 3 months" (section 4). **Expected:** one cadence. **Severity:** annoying (the owner's problem, but a team leader would be told to do both). **Fix:** the fixed register paragraph takes the cadence from the review cycles, or says "at each register review".
8. **Where:** the Incident management policy, where to find it. **Seen:** the document is "Incident management policy, procedure and register"; the procedure is section 8, after six sections of policy, definitions and responsibilities, and the reportable-incident table is section 7. On a phone that is a lot of thumb before the first step. **Expected:** the procedure, or a one-screen "If something happens" box, at the top. **Severity:** annoying (the 30-second test fails; I would search for "000"). **Fix:** a short fixed "If something happens right now" box under the title with the first three steps and a pointer to section 8.
9. **Where:** the incident form on the phone. **Seen:** I can log an incident in under two minutes: two dates default to today, a category list in the Commission's words, "How serious", the participant code with "How codes work" one tap away, the reportable box, and "Notify the Commission by 2026-10-16" appears as soon as I tick reportable. The only thing I need from the owner is the participant's code. **Severity:** passes; one annoyance: "Date it was reported to you" assumes the owner is filling it in ("to you"); as the worker I am reporting, not being reported to. **Fix:** "Date it was reported to the owner" when a worker is signed in, or just "Date it was reported".
10. **Where:** Code of Conduct acknowledgement, section 3 "What this means in our work". **Seen:** a table of seven expectations with a concrete example for a cleaner in someone's home ("We do not read mail, open drawers or cupboards..."; "We record the time we actually spent"). **Severity:** passes; this is the page I would show a new worker first. One gap: the bracket "[Provider to complete: the name of the person a worker should contact when they are unsure what to do, and how to reach them.]" is the one blank a worker needs filled before the first shift. **Fix:** make that blank one of the Finish questions that is required, not optional.
11. **Where:** `/path/audit-day`. **Seen:** (QA 4) "Show me: Read your Governance policy and adopt it." **My view:** I agree; and the registers group reads "Show me: Start your incident register and record each incident on the day it happens" which, on the day, tells me nothing about what to bring. **Severity:** annoying (it is the owner's page).
12. **Where:** Records management policy, section 7 "Retention schedule". **Seen:** every period is "[Provider to complete: retention period]" and the footer says "confirm each retention period with your accountant or a legal adviser". **Expected:** if a participant asks me "how long do you keep notes about me", a number. **Severity:** annoying for me; the domain expert should say whether the Rules give the number (I think they do).
