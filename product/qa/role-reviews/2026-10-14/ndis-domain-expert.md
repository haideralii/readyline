## Findings

1. **Where:** `/registers/incidents/new`, help text under "How serious"; the fixed "Our position on restrictive practices" (safeguarding policy); the escalation table (incident policy). **Seen:** "For an unauthorised restrictive practice: 5 business days to notify the Commission, or 24 hours if the participant was harmed (medium or high)." and "within 5 business days, or within 24 hours if the person was harmed". **Expected:** the Rules' split is by incident kind, not by a harm rating: s 21 gives 5 business days for the unauthorised use of a restrictive practice; the 24 hour duty in s 20 applies when the event is a death, serious injury, abuse or neglect, unlawful sexual or physical contact or sexual misconduct (headings confirmed today; operative wording per the watch's read of 13 Oct 2026, D-545). "Some harm (medium)" is not "serious injury". **Severity:** imprecise (the app and the fixed text turn a category rule into a severity rule; a provider with a "medium" harm rating would over-report, which is safe, but the words claim a rule the Rules do not state). **Fix:** say "within 5 business days; within 24 hours if the use also caused a serious injury or is itself abuse or neglect (Incident Rules ss 20 and 21)"; keep the app's conservative default but word it the same way. UNVERIFIED today: the exact s 20 list, pending a full read of F2018L00633.

4. **Where:** the self-assessment, section 5 "Items to prepare before the audit". **Seen:** "The records listed below are what an auditor asks to see, in the order they ask" followed by twelve records including a delegations register, a corporate risk register, a participant risk register, a staff matrix and an onboarding checklist. **Expected:** for a verification provider the audit is a desk review against the four verification standards (Human resource management, Incident management, Complaints management, Risk management: `data/sources/verification.txt`, Commission verification module page read 11 Oct 2026; `types-of-audits.txt`: "a verification audit is for low risk, low complexity supports and services"). The Practice Standards for those four outcomes do not require a delegations register, a staff matrix or an onboarding checklist; those come from the auditor research (`product/research/webinars/2026-10-14-what-auditors-look-for.md`), which is a hint, not a rule. **Severity:** imprecise (true that an auditor may ask; false that these are "what an auditor asks to see" for this provider's pathway, and the sentence does not distinguish). **Fix:** two lists: the records the applicable standards name (from the dataset's evidence items) and "records auditors often ask for", labelled as such; for verification, the second list should be short.
5. **Where:** `/registers`, intro. **Seen:** "What an auditor samples at the mid-term audit and at renewal." **Expected:** a mid-term audit "is for providers who initially completed a certification audit, and is completed 18 months into your registration period" (`data/sources/types-of-audits.txt`, Commission, read 11 Oct 2026); this provider is verification and the result page says "Mid-term audit: Does not apply". **Severity:** imprecise. **Fix:** "at the mid-term audit (certification providers) and at renewal".
6. **Where:** privacy and information management policy, document control and policy statement. **Seen:** "the Privacy Act 1988 and the NDIS Code of Conduct require us to respect the privacy of the people we support" and "Standards it supports: the Privacy Act 1988...". **Expected:** the Code of Conduct (element 2, respect the privacy of people with disability) applies to every provider; the Privacy Act's application to a sole trader turns on the small business exemption and whether the business provides a health service or holds health information (CLAUDE.md 4.5 records the exemption; the OAIC is the authority and is not an NDIS source, so this is not a "wrong"). **Severity:** imprecise. **Fix:** "the NDIS Code of Conduct requires us to respect privacy, and we follow the Australian Privacy Principles whether or not the Privacy Act binds us" (the brief's own wording in 4.5 supports this).
7. **Where:** records management policy, section 7 "Retention schedule". **Seen:** every period is "[Provider to complete: retention period]" with "[Provider to confirm against the NDIS Incident Management and Reportable Incidents Rules]" and the like. **Expected:** the Incident Rules s 25 "Record keeping" and the Complaints Rules s 10 "Documentation, record keeping and statistics" (headings confirmed today) set the periods; my recollection is 7 years for both, and the worker screening and financial periods come from other instruments. **Severity:** unsourced (UNVERIFIED today: the operative text was not reachable; the pack is right not to invent a number, but the product should supply the sourced one rather than send a sole trader to "your accountant or a legal adviser"). **Fix:** the NDIS watch reads ss 12 and 25 of F2018L00633 and s 10 of F2018L00634 and the retention table takes its sourced rows from `state-reference`-style fixed data with the read date.
8. **Where:** incident policy, section 7 "Reportable incidents" (fixed) and the escalation table. **Seen:** "The categories and the timeframes are set by the NDIS (Incident Management and Reportable Incidents) Rules 2018 and the Commission's guidance, which we keep with this procedure and follow as published." then the table with "Within 24 hours of becoming aware (Incident Rules s 20), with the full report within 5 business days" and "Within 5 business days, in writing (Incident Rules s 21)". **Expected:** this. The section numbers match today's table of contents; the kinds and windows match the watch's read of 13 Oct 2026. **Severity:** none for the table; imprecise for the sentence "which we keep with this procedure": the pack does not include the Commission's list, and section 7 tells the worker to "check the Commission's current list the same day" without saying where it is. **Fix:** name the Commission's reportable incidents page (fixed text with URL and read date) so the lint can allow it.
9. **Where:** worker screening and recruitment procedure, section 6 "Worker screening clearance register" (fixed template). **Seen:** columns "Clearance type and number | Expiry | Next check" and the drafted bracket "enter the clearance number and expiry date in the worker screening clearance register". **Expected:** the product's own rule is that no clearance numbers are stored (D-056, REQ-009), and the app's worker checks register asks for the kind, the issue date and the expiry, not the number. The Worker Screening Rules require a provider to ensure clearances and keep records of them (UNVERIFIED today which record elements). **Severity:** imprecise (pack and product disagree on what the register holds; neither is wrong against a source I can cite today). **Fix:** align the template with the app's columns; if the Rules require the number, the app should store it, not the other way round.
10. **Where:** self-assessment, section 2. **Seen:** "Because we do not use restrictive practices, the behaviour support standards do not apply to us." **Expected:** the supplementary modules (Specialist behaviour support; Implementing behaviour support plans) apply by registration group, not by whether a provider uses restrictive practices; a 0120 provider holds neither module, which is the true reason (`data/sources/registration-groups.txt`, read 11 Oct 2026). **Severity:** imprecise. **Fix:** "Our registration group does not carry the behaviour support modules, and we do not use restrictive practices."
