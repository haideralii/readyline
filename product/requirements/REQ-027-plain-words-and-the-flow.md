## Why

- Haider, 15 Oct: "if the vocabulary of these audits were easy they would have done it in the first place". A support worker who does not like paperwork meets "certification", "module", "quality indicator" and "registration group" on the result page before anything else, and leaves if a word stops them (CLAUDE.md section 1, the one-step-at-a-time rule).
- REQ-025 fixed the structure (lists, the stepper, the 45-word paragraph) and gave every term one meaning in `glossary.json`. What is not done: a sentence rule a writer can keep (the lint fails only at 35 words), a check that every term on a page is explained on that page, and the order of the result page measured against what a first-time reader has to know.
- The research REQ-025 cites says people scan and stop at the first thing they do not understand (NN/g); the Commission's own guidance is written for providers who already know the words, so ours must not be.

## Functional requirements

Session A, the rules (deterministic; no hallway evidence needed)

- FR-1 Sentence length. `src/lib/copy-lint/rules.ts` gains the per-file average: `lintFile` (or the test that calls `lintText` per file) reports `averageSentenceWords` over this population only: sentences inside `p`, `dd`, `li` and `blockquote` blocks, the copy modules' and content files' strings of ten words or more, and the guides' paragraphs; headings, `summary`, button labels and text attributes are not counted (finding 1). It fails a customer page, copy module or content file whose average passes 25 words, beside the 35-word hard limit that stays. Allow-listed constants are left out of the average. The Dev measures the real averages for that population on the result page, the pricing page and one guide before the rule lands and reports them in the evidence (question 2); if the result page is over 25, the shortening is Session A's work. The rule applies to the files the lint already walks (`tests/copy-lint.test.ts`: the app pages, the copy modules, the content JSON, the guides) and to nothing new.
- FR-2 Glossary presence. The lint learns the glossary: for every customer-facing page file, every glossary `term` that appears in the text the lint extracts from it (`inlineText`, the copy modules' string literals, the JSON strings, the guides' paragraphs; never the raw source, so imports, comments and `PAGE_TERMS` do not match; finding 2) must have its `meaning` reach that page through `glossarySentence`, `glossaryMeaning`, the shared disclosure component with that id, or a `{{glossary:<id>}}` token; a verbatim copy of a meaning is not a way (D-2232 and `glossary.test.ts` refuse it; finding 12). Matching is case-insensitive and whole-word over the accepted forms per term, listed in the test as data: the term and its plural for every noun ("module", "modules"; "registration group", "registration groups"; "quality indicator", "quality indicators"; "Practice Standard", "Practice Standards"), the verb forms for "adopt" ("adopt", "adopts", "adopted", "adopting"); "standards" alone and "modular" are not matches (finding 3). A copy module (`home-copy.ts`, `derive.ts`, `payments/copy.ts`) is checked through the page that renders it, by a map of module to page in the test, so `derive.ts`'s nine terms are satisfied by the checklist page's disclosure (finding 13). Skipped: a term whose glossary entry carries a `verify` field (D-2705; finding 11), the content JSON files (checked where they render; finding 30), the legal pages, the guides' sources lists and the glossary file itself.
