## Why (user and business outcome)

An auditor's worker sample is the fastest way to a non-conformity: "show me three worker files" and the newest worker's file is missing the screening check, the Code of Conduct acknowledgement, the induction or the orientation module, because nobody wrote down what a new worker needs before the first shift. Today the product holds a staff matrix and a per-worker onboarding checklist (REQ-018 FR-9), worker clearances with reminders (REQ-018), invitations and a worker role (REQ-020 FR-4), but they are separate screens with no sequence and the worker cannot do their own part. This REQ extends what exists into one sequence per worker that the owner and the worker tick together, with the dates and numbers an auditor samples, the two lists the Worker Screening Rules require (as far as the product can hold them, D-2406 and D-2408), and one export per worker. It is the third reason to keep the AUD 39 subscription.

- W-1 Clearance. A provider in a participating jurisdiction "must only allow a worker to engage in a risk assessed role, if the worker has a clearance" (s 13(a)); a sole-trader provider must hold one themselves (s 13(c)). A clearance lasts up to five years (the primer line 56, the Commission's renewal FAQ; Victoria "5 years from the date of the notice", WA "valid for five years across Australia", NT "five years, unless revoked", unit pages read 16 Oct 2026); the expiry is typed from the outcome notice or the database entry, never derived (finding 52). The provider verifies the application in the NDIS Worker Screening Database (s 15(4): "in accordance with any applicable requirements of the NDIS worker screening law of the participating jurisdiction"; the units put that act in the database, Victoria within 30 days) and records the date (finding 49). A "risk assessed role" is s 5's: (a) a key personnel role, (b) a role whose normal duties include the direct delivery of specified supports or services, (c) a role whose normal duties are likely to require more than incidental contact with a person with disability, with s 6's examples (equipment delivery with training is more than incidental; the back-office accountant is not); a registered provider who is an individual is a worker (note to s 11); so the owner-as-worker is risk assessed by definition, and key personnel by paragraph (a) "a key personnel role of a person or an entity" (NDIS Act 2013 s 11A(1), compilation No. 18 of 18 October 2023, read 16 Oct 2026 by the reviewer: "(a) a member of the group of persons who is responsible for the executive decisions of the person or entity; (b) any other person who has authority or responsibility for (or significant influence over) planning, directing or controlling the activities of the person or entity"; a sole trader is the person responsible for their own executive decisions, so paragraph (a) fits the owner's own row; the current compilation's page returned the table of contents, so a later amendment is UNVERIFIED; finding 117); the owner's own row is forced risk assessed and its paragraph is asked like every other row's, with (a) offered first (findings 43 and 75). The ask names the NDIS Worker Screening Database's "Eligible to work" field as what the owner checks and dates (the Commission's worker screening page and sa.gov.au's verify page, read 16 Oct 2026; finding 121).
- W-1a The pending exception, s 14(1)(a), all four together: the person "is in the process of obtaining a clearance"; "is appropriately supervised by a person with a clearance"; the provider "is implementing a risk management plan developed and maintained in accordance with Division 3"; and the law of the jurisdiction allows it. "In the process" is defined by s 15: it starts only when the screening unit has issued a written notice confirming the application, the provider has confirmed to the unit that the person is or will be its worker, the provider has seen the notice and has recorded the application number as s 18 requires (s 15(3)); s 15(2) has seven limbs and the person is never "in the process" under four of them (finding 70): (a) "if the person has applied ... on at least one prior occasion, and on the most recent prior occasion the person was subject to an exclusion"; (b) a prior clearance "revoked"; (c) "on any day after the application ... is withdrawn by the person"; (d) "on any day after the application ... is closed"; and it ends under (e) an interim bar, (f) supervision stopping and (g) the plan not being implemented, or on the decision. Two state tests apply, not one (finding 45): s 14(1)(a)(iv), "the law of the participating jurisdiction in which the person provides supports or services ... allows the person to engage in a risk assessed role while ... in the process of obtaining a clearance" (the state where the work is done); and s 14A(1)(b), the exception is off where "the law of the participating jurisdiction in which the application is submitted prohibits a person from engaging in a risk assessed role while ... in the process" and, s 14A(1)(c), "the person does not have an acceptable check that applies in that participating jurisdiction", where s 14A(2) keys the acceptable check to the state the participant is served in ("(a) if the person is providing services to a participant in New South Wales—the person has an acceptable NSW check or an acceptable aged care provider check; ... (c) ... in Victoria—the person is allowed ... because of subsection 25(3) ..."), so the escape is per work state, not per application state (finding 71). A worker who applied in Victoria ("no clearance, no start", vic.gov.au read 16 Oct 2026) or Queensland ("can't start work until you have been issued a clearance", workerscreening.qld.gov.au read 16 Oct 2026) and serves a NSW participant is barred by s 14A whatever NSW allows, unless they hold the check s 14A(2)(a) names for NSW. A sole trader's own pending clearance falls under s 14(2)(a), which needs supervision by "a person with a clearance", any person, not necessarily a worker of the provider (finding 35). s 14 holds five exceptions in all: (1)(a) pending with supervision and the plan; (1)(b) the Part 4 transitional arrangements (an acceptable NSW or SA check, or a state registration during its transition period, ss 23 to 29); (1)(c) the supervised secondary-school student; (2)(a) and (2)(b) the same two for the individual provider (finding 47). The product gates (1)(a) and (2)(a) only; the other three are the owner's own call, recordable as a code and dates (FR-6a). s 15(2)(f): the person is not in the process "at any time the person is not being supervised in accordance with the requirements of section 14", so a supervisor's own clearance expiring or being suspended ends the exception that day (finding 50).
- W-1b The Division 3 plan: ss 11 and 12 require a written list of risk assessed roles and a written risk management plan for people with disability while a worker is engaged in a risk assessed role and in the process of obtaining a clearance. s 12(2): the plan "must: (a) identify and describe each risk to a person with disability for whom the registered NDIS provider provides supports or services; and (b) explain how each risk will be managed"; s 12(3): reviewed after a reportable incident involving a worker and "(a) if the Commissioner requires the registered NDIS provider to do so" (findings 72 and 95). A fixed section with the account's supports filled in describes no person's risks; the product holds those by code in the participant risk register (REQ-018 FR-2), so the section is complete only with that register's rows, taken as a snapshot at render (FR-4a, D-2421, D-2424). s 12(1) protects people "while any workers or personnel are: (a) engaged in a risk assessed role ...; and (b) in the process of obtaining a clearance", and s 12(4) says the provider "must implement the risk management plan, unless otherwise agreed by the Commissioner": the section says the plan is implemented from the day the first exception starts, so end code (g) has a start to measure from (finding 136).
