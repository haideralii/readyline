# REQ-024: Worker onboarding: the worker file an auditor samples, from invitation to first shift

Status: draft, fourth version (PO, 16 Oct 2026 03:30; the second pass of the re-review under D-2222, `product/qa/req-reviews/2026-10-15-req-024-rereview.md` findings 31 to 55, verdict "Not ready", every finding answered below with D-2410 to D-2416; Worker Screening Rules F2021C00788 verified by the reviewer as the latest compilation; goes back to the reviewer once more as D-2215 requires; every session waits for REQ-020 to be on `main`)
Phase: 6 Priority: should Owner: Dev

## Why (user and business outcome)

An auditor's worker sample is the fastest way to a non-conformity: "show me three worker files" and the newest worker's file is missing the screening check, the Code of Conduct acknowledgement, the induction or the orientation module, because nobody wrote down what a new worker needs before the first shift. Today the product holds a staff matrix and a per-worker onboarding checklist (REQ-018 FR-9), worker clearances with reminders (REQ-018), invitations and a worker role (REQ-020 FR-4), but they are separate screens with no sequence and the worker cannot do their own part. This REQ extends what exists into one sequence per worker that the owner and the worker tick together, with the dates and numbers an auditor samples, the two lists the Worker Screening Rules require (as far as the product can hold them, D-2406 and D-2408), and one export per worker. It is the third reason to keep the AUD 39 subscription.

## Scope (in / out)

In, as three sessions, all after REQ-020 is on `main`:

- Session A, the onboarding sequence: `staff_matrix`, `onboarding_checklists` and `worker_clearances` extended (not replaced), one new `onboarding_items` table for the mutable state and one `risk_assessed_roles` table for the s 17 list (D-2416), the owner's views, the worker file export, the two Rules lists with the one-time fill, the privacy policy diff.
- Session B, the worker's own steps through the worker role: the worker completes what is theirs and the owner confirms; the acknowledgement rows; the invitation's first steps.
- Session C, keeping it current: the audit-day and checklist states, the supervision rows, the period filter and the pack (after REQ-023 Session B), ending a worker, the pack's two audiences.

Out: payroll, rostering, contracts, award interpretation, HR integrations; identity documents or certificates as files; a worker's performance notes; anything that names a participant; storing worker names, dates of birth and addresses (D-2406); labour-hire personnel (the s 13(b) contract and the s 19 records, review finding 18); the fill-at-download of every generated document, which is its own REQ after REQ-026 (D-2409).

The Worker Screening Rules, F2021C00788 (the latest compilation, verified by the reviewer 15 Oct 2026):

- W-1 Clearance. A provider in a participating jurisdiction "must only allow a worker to engage in a risk assessed role, if the worker has a clearance" (s 13(a)); a sole-trader provider must hold one themselves (s 13(c)). A clearance lasts up to five years (the primer line 56, the Commission's renewal FAQ; Victoria "5 years from the date of the notice", WA "valid for five years across Australia", NT "five years, unless revoked", unit pages read 16 Oct 2026); the expiry is typed from the outcome notice or the database entry, never derived (finding 52). The provider verifies the application in the NDIS Worker Screening Database (s 15(4): "in accordance with any applicable requirements of the NDIS worker screening law of the participating jurisdiction"; the units put that act in the database, Victoria within 30 days) and records the date (finding 49). A "risk assessed role" is s 5's: (a) a key personnel role, (b) a role whose normal duties include the direct delivery of specified supports or services, (c) a role whose normal duties are likely to require more than incidental contact with a person with disability, with s 6's examples (equipment delivery with training is more than incidental; the back-office accountant is not); a registered provider who is an individual is a worker (note to s 11); so key personnel and the owner-as-worker are risk assessed by definition (finding 43).
- W-1a The pending exception, s 14(1)(a), all four together: the person "is in the process of obtaining a clearance"; "is appropriately supervised by a person with a clearance"; the provider "is implementing a risk management plan developed and maintained in accordance with Division 3"; and the law of the jurisdiction allows it. "In the process" is defined by s 15: it starts only when the screening unit has issued a written notice confirming the application, the provider has confirmed to the unit that the person is or will be its worker, the provider has seen the notice and has recorded the application number as s 18 requires (s 15(3)); it ends when supervision stops or the plan is not implemented (s 15(2)(f) and (g)), on an interim bar (s 15(2)(e)), or on the decision. Two state tests apply, not one (finding 45): s 14(1)(a)(iv), "the law of the participating jurisdiction in which the person provides supports or services ... allows the person to engage in a risk assessed role while ... in the process of obtaining a clearance" (the state where the work is done); and s 14A(1)(b), the exception is off where "the law of the participating jurisdiction in which the application is submitted prohibits a person from engaging in a risk assessed role while ... in the process" and the person holds no acceptable check under s 14A(2) and Part 4 (the state of the application). A worker who applied in Victoria ("no clearance, no start", vic.gov.au read 16 Oct 2026) and serves a NSW participant is barred by s 14A whatever NSW allows. A sole trader's own pending clearance falls under s 14(2)(a), which needs supervision by "a person with a clearance", any person, not necessarily a worker of the provider (finding 35). s 14 holds five exceptions in all: (1)(a) pending with supervision and the plan; (1)(b) the Part 4 transitional arrangements (an acceptable NSW or SA check, or a state registration during its transition period, ss 23 to 29); (1)(c) the supervised secondary-school student; (2)(a) and (2)(b) the same two for the individual provider (finding 47). The product gates (1)(a) and (2)(a) only; the other three are the owner's own call, recordable as a code and dates (FR-6a). s 15(2)(f): the person is not in the process "at any time the person is not being supervised in accordance with the requirements of section 14", so a supervisor's own clearance expiring or being suspended ends the exception that day (finding 50).
- W-1b The Division 3 plan: ss 11 and 12 require a written list of risk assessed roles and a written risk management plan for people with disability while a worker is engaged in a risk assessed role and in the process of obtaining a clearance, reviewed after a reportable incident involving a worker. No document in the catalogue is that plan today (review finding 14): this REQ adds it as a section of the worker screening and recruitment procedure (FR-4a).
- W-1c The records, Part 3: a written list of risk assessed roles with the title, the paragraph of the definition that applies, a description, the date assessed and "the name and title of the person who assessed the role" (s 17(3)), updated within 20 business days of a new or reclassified role (s 17(2)); a written list of each worker in a risk assessed role with "the worker's full name, date of birth and address", the roles, for an exception the section relied on, its start and end dates and the supervisor's name, and otherwise the application number, the check number, the outcome and its expiry, and whether the clearance is subject to a decision to suspend or revoke it or any other decision that stops the worker, and the nature of that decision (s 18(3)); kept in a form that lets an auditor "determine which workers were engaged in risk assessed roles with the provider on any given day in the seven years prior" (s 20); the provider must keep a copy of any record relating to an interim bar, a suspension, an exclusion or its own action on them (s 18(4)), of misconduct allegations against a cleared worker and the action taken (s 18(5)), and the personnel contract records (s 19); every record in the Part kept 7 years from the date it is made (s 21). The seven-year duty is the provider's; the product's part is D-2406 and the retention rule in Data; the s 18(4) and (5) copies and the s 19 records are the owner's paper file (finding 48).
- W-2 Working with children check, where this worker's role is child-related work in the provider's state: a state scheme, and in two states the same registration as the NDIS check: in Tasmania the NDIS check is an endorsement on the Registration to Work with Vulnerable People (UNVERIFIED, snippets only) and in the ACT "NDIS worker checks and clearances are part of the Working with Vulnerable People (WWVP) scheme" (Access Canberra, read 16 Oct 2026); Queensland takes one combined application for the blue card and the NDIS clearance (qld.gov.au, read 16 Oct 2026, detail UNVERIFIED); NSW, VIC, WA, SA and NT run two separate checks, and the NT Ochre Card lasts two years, not five (finding 46). The item's wording branches on `combined_scheme` in `data/state-screening.json` (FR-2a); the trigger is the worker's role, not the account's having a child participant (finding 53).
- W-3 NDIS Code of Conduct: every worker is bound by it; the acknowledgement document in the pack (REQ-005) is the record. Source: NDIS (Code of Conduct) Rules 2018, s 5 "Persons covered by the NDIS Code of Conduct", compilation F2024C00048 (the text page returned the table of contents only, 16 Oct 2026; the watch file records the read when it opens; finding 42).
- W-4 NDIS Worker Orientation Module. The Practice Standards' quality indicators in the repository (`data/standards.json`, dataset 1.2.0, verified 11 Oct 2026) say "completion of the mandatory NDIS worker orientation program" (core, human resource management) and "Workers complete mandatory NDIS orientation module" (verification module): the item's source line quotes the indicator and says "mandatory". VERIFY only the module's current title ("Quality, Safety and You" is the primer's name).
- W-5 Induction against the provider's own induction procedure (REQ-005), recorded in the induction and training register (REQ-018). The source line also carries the WHS duty to give "information, training, instruction or supervision" (model Work Health and Safety Act s 19(3)(f) in every state but Victoria; Occupational Health and Safety Act 2004 (Vic) s 21(2)(e)), the sections for the lawyer to confirm, with the state's Act name from the state file (D-2223 item 5; finding 54).
- W-6 Read-and-acknowledge of the mandatory policies: today's `policies-walked-through` item names the Code of Conduct, incident, complaints, privacy and safeguarding policies (`src/lib/registers/onboarding-items.ts`); that list is the "mandatory policies" everywhere this REQ says it. No acknowledgement rows exist today (`policy_circulations` is owner-recorded): the rows are new work in Session B.
- W-7 Qualifications and training the supports require (first aid, CPR, medication, manual handling, the high-intensity skills descriptors, PBS capability): derived from the account's modules; the product names what the module's quality indicators name and marks the rest "your call". VERIFY against the Practice Standards' quality indicators (REQ-001 mapping).
- W-8 Supervision and probation: the provider's own cadence (the governance and HR fixed text); the auditor expects a record that supervision happened; `supervision-due` already fires monthly from `staff_matrix.last_supervision_on` (REQ-018 FR-8) and stays the only supervision reminder.
- W-9 Restrictive practices: a worker who may use one needs the account's behaviour support arrangements and the state authorisation pathway in their induction (D-2223 item 3). VERIFY per state.
- W-10 Other checks a real provider holds: a police check and a driver licence for transport supports; dated `worker_clearances` rows (FR-5).
