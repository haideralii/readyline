## Why (user and business outcome)
A provider who pays us must get their data back after a lost database or a leaked key, and must hear from us when something goes wrong (internal wording, D-2877). The threat model promises backups with a tested restore, error tracking without PII, an uptime check and an incident runbook (lines 45, 53, 54); none exists. This REQ closes the four on free tiers, every live step Haider's.

## Domain rules that apply
- CLAUDE.md 4.5 and the threat model: no participant data, free text, token or email in any log or event; Australian region at rest, so a copy held elsewhere is stated on the privacy page (FR-7).
- Notifiable Data Breaches scheme (Privacy Act 1988, Part IIIC): assess within 30 days; where serious harm is likely, notify the OAIC and the affected people. Readyline may sit under the small-business exemption; the runbook follows the scheme anyway (threat model line 54). The Commission's reportable-incident rules bind providers; Readyline is not one (D-2878).

- D-2207: 2,000 Actions minutes a month; a CI run about 45; `generate-kick.yml` already costs about 1,440. Every schedule here states its minutes.

## Security and privacy requirements
- SR-1 No event, artifact name, workflow log, issue, audit `target` or runbook line carries a secret value, email, name, free text or document content; `beforeSend` is unit-tested with markers.
- SR-2 The dump is piped into the encryptor where possible; a plaintext file on disk is deleted in the same step; the artifact is the encrypted file alone; `::add-mask::` on the passphrase and the URL.
- SR-3 Minimum permissions: `contents: read` everywhere; `issues: write` only on the kick job; nothing writes to the repository. The repository's visibility is a Haider check in `LAUNCH.md` beside the backup row; the issue's title and body carry status codes and times only, never a URL with a token or an address.
- SR-4 The uptime route writes one row; the backup insert is one statement; neither reads data.
- SR-5 The rehearsal uses a committed fixture passphrase; the production one never appears in CI. A DSN lets anyone send events; it rotates with the rest.
