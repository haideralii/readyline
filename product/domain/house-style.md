# House style for generated documents

Owner: PO. Used verbatim in the generator's system prompt (REQ-005) and by the terminology lint. Version 2, 14 Oct 2026 (version 1 was 12 Oct 2026; version 2 adds the sections marked v2 from the auditor research of 14 Oct 2026).

## Voice
Write as the provider, in first person plural ("we", "our workers"), to be read by the provider's own workers and by an auditor. Calm, specific, plain. No marketing language, no reassurance, no hedging. Every sentence should tell someone what to do, who does it, or when.

## Reading level
Target a Year 8 reading level (Flesch-Kincaid grade 8; warn above 9, fail above 11). Short sentences, one idea each. Prefer common words: "use" not "utilise", "start" not "commence", "tell" not "advise", "help" not "assist" except in the Commission's own class names. Define a term the first time it appears if a new worker might not know it.

## Terminology (lint-enforced)
Use "participant" (never client, customer, patient, consumer, resident). Use "worker" (never staff member, employee, carer, support worker as a role title unless the class name uses it). Use "provider" or the trading name for the business. Use "the Commission" for the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission after the first full mention. Use "NDIS" only descriptively. Australian spelling (organise, programme is not used; use "program"). Dates as 12 October 2026. No exclamation marks. No "please".

## Claims (lint-enforced, CLAUDE.md 4.3)
Never: guaranteed, audit-proof, fully compliant, approved by, endorsed by, Commission-approved, 100%. Say what we do, not that we comply: "We review this policy every 12 months" rather than "This policy complies with the standard".

## Structure
Every policy and procedure: 1 Purpose (two sentences: what this document is for and which standard it supports), 2 Scope (who and what it covers), 3 What we do (numbered steps or short subsections; responsibilities in a table with Role, Does what, When), 4 Records we keep (list the registers and files by name), 5 Related documents, 6 Review (review cycle, owner, next review date). Headings are numbered. Tables for responsibilities and timeframes. Bullet lists only for genuinely parallel items; never a bullet list of full paragraphs.

## Outcome, commitment, procedure (v2, lint-enforced)
The quality indicator is the outcome; our policy states our commitment; our procedure says how we meet it: which role does what, by when, and on which record. Never restate a quality indicator as a sentence of the policy. A sentence that repeats the indicator's words is no work done; the auditor wants to read what we do.

## Actors are roles (v2, lint-enforced)
Every step names the role that does it, taken from the responsibilities table: the owner, the key management person, the team leader, the worker on shift, the incident reporter. Never "staff", "the team", "someone" or a person's name. "Staff" appears only inside a name the Commission or this pack uses, such as the staff matrix.

## Time frames are numbers (v2, lint-enforced)
Every time frame is a number: "within 24 hours", "before the end of the shift", "every 12 months", "within 5 business days". The words "timely", "promptly", "as soon as practicable", "regularly", "periodically" and "appropriate" are banned unless a number follows within a few words. A statutory time frame is quoted only in the fixed section that holds the rule; a tailored section refers to that section.

## Records we keep (v2, lint-enforced)
"Records we keep" lists only registers, forms and documents that exist in this pack or in the Readyline registers, by their exact names: the incident register, the complaints register, the worker checks register, the policy review register, the corporate risk register, the participant risk register, the conflict of interest register, the delegations register, the insurances and vehicles register, the improvement register, the record of decisions, the staff matrix, the onboarding checklist, the document control register, and the forms and templates in this pack. Never name a record that does not exist.

## Words we use (v2)
A procedure for complaints, incidents, open disclosure or boundaries gives workers the exact words in a short "Words we use" callout. Open disclosure: "I am sorry this has happened" and "I am sorry this has not turned out as expected", an apology that admits no liability. Boundaries: say plainly what we do not do.

## If X happens (v2)
Each procedure has at least one "If X happens, do Y" step for its foreseeable moment: a participant complains, an incident occurs, a medical emergency on the participant's list, a restrictive practice is used in an emergency, a worker's clearance lapses.

## Restrictive practices (v2)
Every pack takes a position on restrictive practices, including a provider that does not use them: what we do if one is used in an emergency, what counts as an unintended practice (a locked door, a withheld item, a held arm), what the worker does and who they tell, the same day.

## Two levels of risk (v2)
Risk is managed at two levels: the corporate risk register, which names participants as one of its areas, and the participant risk register with one row per participant. A policy that manages risk at the organisation level only is a common cause of lost registration.

## Tailoring
Tailored sections must use the provider's facts from the questionnaire: trading name, structure, states, support types, worker count band, whether restrictive practices are used. Never invent people's names, addresses, participant details, incident examples with identifying details, or statistics. If a fact is not in the context pack, write the sentence so it does not need it, or write the complete sentence and leave only the missing piece as a blank in square brackets naming the fact, at most four words: [name], [role], [phone or email], [date], [state]. A blank is never an instruction and never a question.

## What never goes in a document
Participant personal information of any kind. Promises about registration or audit outcomes. Legal advice ("you must" about the law is fine when it quotes the Rules; interpreting a provider's specific legal position is not). Readyline marketing.

## Fixed text we always include
Document control table on page one (title, provider, version, approved by, approval date, next review, the standards it supports) and a change log table at the end. The documents are the provider's: the general-information notice and the AI disclosure in `CLAUDE.md` 4.2 and 4.3 sit at the point of delivery (the download page, the readiness summary's first page and the cover note in the zip), never inside a policy. The footer reads "Draft prepared with Readyline" until the provider adopts the document, then carries only the title, version and page number.
