# What auditors look for: knowledge from sector webinars (PO, 14 Oct 2026)

## Source tier (PO rule, 14 Oct 2026)

Official sources always lead: the NDIS Act and Rules on legislation.gov.au, then the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission's own pages, PDFs and the NDIS Practice Standards and Quality Indicators, then the Commission's approved quality auditor guidance. Webinars, consultants' videos and competitor blogs are secondary: they tell us what auditors ask for on the day and how providers fail, never what the rule is. Where a video and an official document disagree, the official document wins and the video item is dropped. Every item below is tagged OFFICIAL (checked against the Commission or legislation), PRACTICE (an auditor's or consultant's account of what happens at audit; useful, not a rule) or COMMERCIAL (a competitor's claim). The weekly NDIS watch (scheduled task) re-checks the OFFICIAL items.

## A. How an audit actually runs (PRACTICE unless tagged)

Audit types a provider meets: provisional (first registration, no participants yet; documents, forms, credentials only), the first audit with participants (auditors interview participants and workers), mid-term (about 18 months, certification only), renewal every 3 years, and a conditional or follow-up audit when the Commission sets conditions. OFFICIAL for the cycle; PRACTICE for what each feels like.

Auditors triangulate three things in roughly equal weight: the policies and procedures with their forms and templates; file audits (participant files, worker files, registers); and interviews with workers and participants. Perfect documents with empty registers fail; good practice with nothing written down also fails because it cannot be evidenced.

Participants must stay on the interview list. An auditor may skip someone who would be distressed, but a provider cannot remove participants from the list. If an auditor says otherwise, report it to JAS-ANZ (the accreditation body for audit companies) and the Commission.

Audit pricing is a daily rate times a methodology fixed in the Rules; the daily rate is the provider's to ask for and the provider can challenge the day count. Quotes for a sole support coordinator's renewal ranged $5,000 to $7,000 in 2026. A nurse is added (and charged) only when Module 1 is in scope.

A failed audit can put the provider on a 90-day review cycle, recorded on the public register as a compliance order.

## B. What the auditor asks to see (the evidence list)

Policies and procedures are one third. The rest is records. Every item an auditor asked for in the sources, grouped:

Governance and business: governance structure; ABN and business registration; valid insurance certificates (public liability, professional indemnity, workers compensation); a document control register (every policy with created, reviewed, version, next review); a record of decisions or meeting minutes; a corporate risk register covering emergency and disaster, participant-related, compliance, HR and staffing, organisational sustainability, governance, finance, legislation change and data security, every field filled and a review date on every row; a conflict of interest register (staff and participants) with review dates; a delegations register (who acts when the owner or key person is away); an internal audit program between external audits; a continuous improvement register fed by incidents, complaints, feedback and internal audits, with the loop closed (trial, review, adopt, update the policy, circulate).

Participants: signed service agreement; consent; a support plan linked to NDIS goals with review dates; progress notes; a participant risk assessment and a participant risk register (one line per participant, aggregated across all participants, review date); for each participant a medical emergency list with the agreed action for each; an onboarding checklist signed and dated; a transition or exit plan when supports end.

Workers: proof of identity; employment agreement; qualifications; NDIS worker screening clearance (recorded; supervised until cleared); mandatory training with expiry (NDIS worker orientation module, first aid, manual handling, medication where relevant); a staff matrix combining the above with the risk-assessed role flag; signed onboarding checklist; supervision records (at least monthly in the sources); a 12-month performance appraisal that includes policy knowledge; police checks for finance roles; ABN contractors carrying their own insurance.

The big three: incident register (every incident, investigation, what changed, reportable flag standing out, root cause analysis for reportables); complaints register (distinct from feedback, with resolution time frames); risk registers (corporate and participant).

Site and assets (SIL and SDA): emergency drills and a disaster plan with a prioritised participant list; vehicle register (rego, insurance, driver licences); asset list; cleaning, sanitising and maintenance records.

## C. How a policy must be written (what a policy auditor rejects)

C1. The quality indicator sets the outcome; the policy states our commitment; the procedure says how, who (a role, never "staff" and never a person's name), when (a real time frame: "within 24 hours", "before the end of the shift", never "timely" or "as soon as practicable"), and with which form or register, named exactly as it exists. A policy that restates the quality indicator verbatim, or twice, is marked as "no work done".

C2. The audit test: "If a worker followed this procedure exactly, would the auditor's evidence be produced?" Every step in What we do should create a record that is listed by name in Records we keep.

C3. Give workers the words. Open disclosure: "I am sorry this has happened" and "I am sorry this has not turned out as expected" (an apology without admitting liability). Boundaries: say what we do not do.

C4. Write "if X happens, do Y" for the foreseeable moments: a participant complains, an incident occurs, a medical emergency on the participant's list, a restrictive practice is used in an emergency, a worker's clearance lapses.

C5. Readability: Year 8 level (a University of Queensland review of behaviour support plans recommended Grade 8 and the sources extend that to all working documents); numbered steps or flowcharts; short role-specific reading packs for workers; policies reachable on a phone in the field.

C6. Separate policy statement from procedure, but keep them in one document so nothing gets lost; add work instructions where a new worker would not know what to do.

C7. No leftover "[insert organisation]" text, no vague "we ensure" sentences, no 26-page documents for a sole trader. The document must match what the provider actually does and name the actual forms; a mismatch is a finding. Policies from another sector's standards (aged care, community health) are not accepted.

C8. Review every policy at least once per audit cycle (18 months); we commit to 12 months. Circulate every change (staff meeting, email, LMS) and record that it was circulated. Induction walks through the relevant policies and is signed and dated; supervision and appraisal check policy knowledge.

C9. Restrictive practices: every provider needs a policy position, including providers not registered for Module 2A, covering emergency use and unintended practices (a locked door, a withheld item).

C10. Risk: the corporate register must name participants as a risk area, and there must be a participant-level register as well; organisation-only risk management is a common cause of lost registration.

## D. The five gaps most likely to cost registration (PRACTICE, consistent across sources)

D1. Incident management without an escalation pathway and without the Commission's notification time frames (24 hours for reportable incidents; 5 business days, 24 hours if harm, for unauthorised restrictive practice; OFFICIAL).
D2. Complaints with no distinction from feedback and no resolution time frames.
D3. Risk management at organisation level only, nothing per participant.
D4. No restrictive practice position.
D5. Worker screening and training policy that does not match the actual records.
