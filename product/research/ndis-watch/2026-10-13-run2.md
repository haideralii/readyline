# NDIS watch: 13 October 2026

First report in this folder; automated research run, no code or data changed. VERIFIED: Commission, legislation.gov.au or state government source read 13 October 2026. UNVERIFIED: secondary source only.

## Summary

1. The Provider Registration Rules add two conditions the primer lacks: an "earlier of" event notification rule (s 13A) and a targeted certification audit within 3 months of a change in ownership (s 13BA).
2. The Securing the NDIS for Future Generations Act 2026 (No. 66, assent 20 August 2026) adds a 7-year duty to keep claim records (s 45B) and an inducement offence (s 73VA, from 20 November 2026).
3. Wave 2 of mandatory registration (personal care, daily living and closed settings from 1 July 2027; all in scope by December 2030) is confirmed by the Department.
4. State-specific sections are written from model memory; the table below gives verified facts (Tasmania and the Northern Territory changed).
5. NDISCompliant has pivoted to a marketplace brand (ProviderQMS); Auditly is the only AI-forward product with published prices. Privacy tranche 2 keeps the small business exemption.

## Proposed changes to the generator

1. **Event notification (s 13A).** Source: Rules 2018, F2026C00527, s 13A. Affects: primer step 7; `governance-policy` outline (new tailored section "Telling the Commission about changes and events"). Primer wording: "Notifying the Commission (Rules s 13A, from 1 July 2026): notice is due by the earlier of when we become aware the event will occur and when it occurs; for a change of ownership, as soon as we know it will occur or take any step toward it." Section brief: "List the s 13A events in plain words and the rule that we tell the Commission through its portal as soon as we know, not after." VERIFIED.

2. **Change-in-ownership audit (s 13BA).** Source: s 13BA (certification providers only; not if already in a mid-term audit; auditor reports within 28 days). Affects: primer step 6; REQ-011 buying-or-selling page. Wording: "Change of ownership (certification providers, Rules s 13BA, from 1 July 2026): a significant change in organisation or governance caused by a change in ownership triggers a targeted certification audit that must start within 3 months, covering governance and operational management and any standard under a corrective action." VERIFIED.

3. **Mid-term audit wording.** Section 13B(4): the audit must "commence no later than 18 months after the beginning of the period for which the provider's registration is in force"; the Commissioner may add standards by written notice (s 13B(5)(c)). Affects: primer step 6. Wording: "mid-term audit that must start no later than 18 months into the registration period (our advice: prepare from month 12), against Part 3 of Schedule 1, any standard previously under a corrective action plan, and any standard the Commissioner names in writing". VERIFIED.

4. **7-year claim records.** New NDIS Act s 45B: keep records relating to claims for payment for 7 years from the claim; civil penalty; from 27 August 2026. Sources: Act No. 66 of 2026 on legislation.gov.au; the Department's provider FAQ (7-year duty and penalty); section and date from Clinic Comply, 8 Oct 2026. Affects: `records-management-policy` fixed retention text; primer step 7. Wording: "We keep records of every claim for payment of NDIS funds, and of the supports they relate to, for 7 years from the date of the claim (NDIS Act s 45B)." VERIFIED (Act and Department); Dev quotes s 45B from the Act before shipping.

5. **Inducement prohibition.** The Act makes offering kickbacks and inducements an offence (Commission media release); s 73VA, from 20 November 2026 (secondary). Affects: `conflict-of-interest-policy` and `conflict-of-interest-in-coordination-procedure` policy-statement briefs. Wording: "We do not offer or accept any payment, gift or benefit to attract or keep a participant, or in return for a referral. From 20 November 2026 this is also an offence under the NDIS Act." VERIFIED that the offence exists; section and date UNVERIFIED until quoted from the Act.

6. **Mandatory registration wave 2.** Department timeline: "Expanded registration requirements will begin to be rolled out from 1 July 2027"; "All providers in scope will need to be registered by December 2030". A consultation on the definitions of the two personal-care classes, run for the Commission by First Peoples Disability Network, closes 20 October 2026 (National Indigenous Times; UNVERIFIED). Affects: primer seed-list note; REQ-011 timeline page. Wording: "Wave 2 (Department timeline, 2026): personal care, daily living and closed-setting supports from 1 July 2027; all in-scope providers registered by December 2030; provider enrolment for everyone from 1 July 2027. Support coordination mandatory registration remains paused." VERIFIED.

7. **Digital platform conditions (s 13D).** Section 13D sets worker-screening evidence and display conditions for class 0137; DSC says from 1 January 2027. Affects: `standards.json` note on 0137 (D-104). Wording: "0137 carries registration conditions in Rules s 13D (worker screening evidence for every person on the platform; banning order and credential checks displayed); modules stay core unless the Commission publishes otherwise." VERIFIED for s 13D; the date UNVERIFIED.

8. **Reportable incident wording in the primer.** Incident Rules: s 20 (within 24 hours), s 21 (other reportable incidents within 5 business days), s 24 (final report on request). The primer seed ("a fuller report within 5 business days") conflates them; `status.ts` (D-902) is right. Affects: primer "Rules the registers must respect". Wording: "Reportable incidents: the 24-hour categories (death, serious injury, abuse or neglect, unlawful sexual or physical contact, sexual misconduct) and unauthorised restrictive practice causing harm go to the Commission within 24 hours of key personnel becoming aware; unauthorised restrictive practice without harm within 5 business days; a final report when the Commission asks (Rules ss 20, 21, 24). Other incidents stay in our register." VERIFIED.

9. **State reference block for the prompt.** The three state-tailored sections are written from model memory; `prompts.ts` passes only state codes. Tasmania's authoriser is now the Senior Practitioner under the 2024 Act (from 1 July 2025), not TASCAT; the NT authoriser is the Senior Practitioner (Dept of Health) under the NDIS (Authorisations) Act 2019 (NT). Affects: new `data/state-reference.json` (source URL and date per fact, as in `standards.json`); `prompts.ts` (a `<<<STATE FACTS>>>` block for the provider's states); the three briefs ("use only the STATE FACTS block; if a state is missing, write the [state] blank, never a body name from memory"). Affects: `fixed-extras.ts` "clearance-register"; worker-checks register empty-state copy. 11. **Name the AI position statement.** The Commission's only published position on providers' AI use is "Position statement: Use of artificial intelligence in development of behaviour support plans" (February 2026). It applies the Code of Conduct privacy duty to any provider using AI, expects that "no personal information of participants is disclosed to AI systems", and flags automated decisions without human oversight. Affects: `ai-use-and-governance-policy` fixed section and `basisReason`; `fixed-extras.ts` line 297. Wording: "The Commission's position statement on AI in behaviour support plans (February 2026) says no participant personal information should go into an AI system and that a person, not the tool, makes every decision about a participant. We apply that rule to every AI and digital tool we use." VERIFIED. Note: the Commission's own AI transparency statement cited in CLAUDE.md 3a could not be re-read this run (UNVERIFIED).

12. **Privacy law.** Tranche 1 is in force; automated decision-making transparency (APP 1.7) applies from 10 December 2026. Affects: `privacy-and-information-management-policy`; Readyline's privacy page. Wording: "We do not use computer programs to make decisions about participants without a person reviewing them.
