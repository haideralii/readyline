# Competitors and go-to-market (PO research, 13 Oct 2026)

Sources are public pages read on 13 Oct 2026; prices are as shown that day (most ex GST unless stated). Claims like "3,000+ providers" are the competitors' own.

## 1. The market in one paragraph

About 26,300 NDIS providers are listed in Australia; roughly 16,800 are registered and about 9,500 are not (ProviderScout, June 2026, cross-checked against the Commission register). Registration is becoming compulsory in waves: SIL and digital-platform providers from 1 July 2026 (unregistered SIL providers had to apply by 1 October 2026 or stop, and new SIL entrants cannot provide SIL while an application is pending; the platform conditions in Rules s 13D start 1 January 2027: Commission explanatory document, June 2026, read 13 October 2026); providers of higher-risk activities (personal care, everyday-living help, closed settings) from 1 July 2027, with every in-scope provider registered by December 2030 (Securing the NDIS for Future Generations Act 2026). The Commission says application volume is already rising. So for the next four years thousands of small, unregistered providers will be forced through the audit Readyline prepares them for. That is the wave to ride.

## 2. Who we compete with

### 2a. Consultants (indirect, the price anchor)
Provider+ (8 offices, "10,000+ providers", bespoke policy packs, free 30-minute consult, local landing pages for every capital city), VCCG, Premium Consultancy Services, Engels Floyd, Become A Provider. Market price for documentation and application support: **AUD 2,500 to 8,000**, 2 to 4 weeks. They win on trust and hand-holding, lose on price and speed. They are also the best content marketers in the niche (cost guides, checklists, city pages, glossaries, auditor directories).

### 2c. Compliance software (indirect now, direct for the subscription later)
ONDIS ($59/month verification, $99 to $899/month certification by staff count, "370+ documents", Compliance Monitor), Effica, Accorda, AuditCore, ClearLine, NDIS Pulse, NDIS Compliant. Register-and-evidence systems for providers with staff; too much for a sole trader, and none generate tailored policies.

### 2d. The one real direct competitor: Auditly (auditly.au)
AI-assisted, questionnaire-driven ("chat-style onboarding": services, groups, states, scale), "Living Policy Engine" that updates when standards change, Policy Health Score, plus a QMS with registers. **Verification suite $695/year, Certification $1,500/year, add-on modules $695/year each, QMS $395/month**, 14-day free trial. Founder Tania Gomez (Provider+), so it inherits a big brand and a podcast audience. Weaknesses: annual subscription for something a sole trader needs once; no visible price for the whole thing until the pricing page; heavy for a one-person business; claims of "10,000+/13,000+ providers" are the consultancy's, not the product's.

## 3. Where Readyline sits
- **Price:** AUD 399 one-off sits between Bluetail ($65, generic) and Provider360 Standard ($670, generic). Below Auditly's first year ($695) with no renewal. Keep it; do not race Bluetail down.
- **Product:** the only offer that (a) decides verification vs certification from answers with the Commission source shown, (b) drafts the tailored sections of every document from those answers with the fixed legal text kept exact, (c) hands over a checklist that reflects what the buyer already has, and (d) is honest that the output is a draft to review. Nobody else does (a)+(b); Auditly does a version of (b) at 1.7x the price per year.
- **Trust gap:** every competitor shows provider counts, star ratings, named testimonials, a phone number, a guarantee, and a free sample. Readyline has none of these yet. The private trial must produce the first three testimonials and the "passed first time" stories.
- **Ethical edge (and a constraint):** no Afterpay, no instalments, no countdown timers, no "audit-proof". Say so on the pricing page; it reads as integrity next to the countdown-timer sellers.

## 4. How they get customers (what is working in this niche)
1. **Cost-question SEO.** "How much does NDIS registration cost", "consultant cost", "audit cost": Provider+, ProviderPass, Veyora, Provider360 all own long guides with tables, FAQs and "last reviewed" dates. These pages are the funnel top.
2. **Free lead magnets for an email:** readiness checklists, sample packs, roadmaps, free service agreement template, free Code of Conduct, "audit checker".
3. **Package finders / quizzes** (Provider360, Veyora): the same job our free questionnaire does, but ours is better because it also explains the audit type with sources.
4. **Local pages:** "NDIS registration consultant Sydney/Melbourne/Brisbane" and per-state pages (Provider+, Veyora).
5. **Community:** Bluetail's Facebook group and YouTube series; Auditly's podcasts and live demos; NDS support-worker network. Sole traders live in Facebook groups.
6. **Partnerships and referrals:** insurance (BizCover), RTO training, auditor directories, accountants and bookkeepers who set up ABNs for new providers.
7. **Urgency:** countdown discounts (Provider Audit Ready), "apply by 1 October" deadline content for SIL, "2027 is coming" pieces for personal-care providers.

## 5. How to out-rank and out-convert them (REQ-011 input)

**Positioning line:** "Answer 20 questions. Get the exact documents your audit needs, written for your business, in about 20 minutes. One price, no subscription, no guarantee games."

**Content plan, in order of search intent (each page: plain English, a table, an FAQ, a "last reviewed" date, the Commission source linked, a free-questionnaire CTA, no gated PDFs):**
1. NDIS registration cost in 2026/27 (application $0, audit ranges, consultant ranges, our price) with a cost calculator that uses the questionnaire's audit-type logic.
2. Verification vs certification: which audit do I need? (our strongest page: the questionnaire answers it live with sources).
3. Mandatory registration timeline 2026 to 2030: who must register when (SIL now, personal care from July 2027), updated as rules change.
4. Per registration-group pages: "Registering for 0120 Household tasks" etc. for all 38 groups, each stating the audit type, the standards, the document list and the price. Nobody has done this well; it is long-tail gold.
5. Per-document pages: "NDIS incident management policy: what it must contain" for each of the 24 documents, with a redacted sample paragraph from a generated draft.
6. Per-state pages (NSW, VIC, QLD, WA, SA, TAS, ACT, NT) with state-specific worker screening links.
7. "What auditors actually ask a sole trader" and "How to fail an NDIS audit" (the mistakes pages do well for Provider+).
8. Comparison pages written straight: "Readyline vs a consultant", "Readyline vs a template pack", "Readyline vs Auditly" with honest tables.

**Technical SEO already in hand:** fast Next.js pages, CSP, a11y pass, clean URLs. Add: sitemap, structured data (FAQPage, Product with price, Organization), per-page meta, canonical domain (buy a real domain; `vercel.app` will never rank as a brand), Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster, an `llms.txt` and plain summaries so AI assistants cite us (Provider+ already adds "summarise in ChatGPT" buttons; answer-engine visibility is the new front).

**Free tools that earn links:** the questionnaire itself as a public "Which NDIS audit do I need?" tool; a registration-group lookup; a cost calculator. Each one is a page people link to from groups and forums.

**Trust assets to build during the trial:** three named testimonials with business names, a "first audits passed" counter that starts honest at 0, a visible phone or booking link, the ABN and the real person's name on About, a Google Business Profile for reviews.

**Distribution beyond search:** one short YouTube/Shorts series ("verification vs certification in 90 seconds"), presence in the main sole-trader Facebook groups as a helpful answerer, not a poster; a referral arrangement with one or two bookkeepers or insurance brokers who see new providers first; a free monthly "what changed at the Commission" email (we already read the rules).

**Pricing page conversions:** show the per-document list for the buyer's own answers (done), a "what you would pay a consultant" comparison line, the refund promise above the fold, and a sample document to download before buying (a redacted generated draft, not a template).

## 6. Do not copy
Countdown timers, strikethrough list prices, Afterpay, "audit-proof", invented provider counts, star ratings without reviews behind them. Our legal pages promise the opposite and the pages that promise these things are the ones providers complain about in groups.
